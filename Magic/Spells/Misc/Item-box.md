# Item Box

The Item Box is a powerful late-game spell that gives a mage a private store of their own, a pocket of magical space that only the caster can reach. It functions as a personal vault carried in the weave of the spell rather than on the body, and for a player heavily invested in magic it offers a kind of convenience and security no physical container can match. It is one of the defining rewards of a committed caster, and a direct answer to the harshest stakes of the world.

## Gameplay Role

The Item Box is a quick, one-handed spell under the [casting rules](../../../Magic.md#casting): each deposit is a short gathering during which the caster must stand still, and plain damage breaks it. It is an advanced spell, unlocked by the light school's foundation spells, [Mending Light](../Light/Mending-light.md) and [Revealing Light](../Light/Revealing-light.md), and it asks for a minimum Spell Power on top of the ranks in both.

The Item Box's value rests on three properties. Its contents are accessible to the caster alone, so what is stored in it is genuinely private and secure. What it holds sits outside the carry weight of [Inventory](../../../Inventory.md), letting a mage keep essentials on hand without being encumbered by them. Above all, its contents are not lost on death: as set out in [Death and Loss](../../../Death-and-loss.md), the Item Box never appears on the corpse, which makes it the one reliable way to carry something into danger without wagering it, and a large part of why the spell is so prized.

The exemption is a reward for the years a caster puts into magic, and it is paid for in full. Its price is a standing drain of mana, a handful of slots that only more upgrade points can widen, and a deposit cast that leaves the caster exposed while it runs. It is a vault for a mage's essentials, not a way to move a warehouse, and nobody can run a caravan out of it. A character who is not a mage and needs to haul goods in bulk has a different answer, set out in [Saddles and Carriages](../../../Saddles-and-carriages.md): a goods saddle or a carriage carries far more than the box ever will, and it does so in the open, where raiders can take it. More capacity, more risk, and no mana bill is the trade a non-mage makes, and it is the reason the two answers can sit side by side.

## Upgrade Directions

The spell grows through invested upgrade points, and its upgrades are build choices rather than flat increases. A caster can expand the number of slots the box holds, trading depth in this spell against everything else their points could buy; reduce the spell's continuous mana drain to make long-term storage sustainable; or unlock preservation, which keeps food and other perishables from spoiling inside the box and turns it into the ideal larder for the long survival expeditions of [Survival](../../../Survival.md). Each direction shapes the box toward a different role, the deep vault, the always-on convenience, or the expedition pantry.

## Trade-Offs

The Item Box is kept in check by mana, and the price has three parts. Opening it to reach the contents costs a significant amount of mana up front, and while items remain stored the spell drains mana continuously, with the drain scaling by the number and type of items held, so the more valuable the hoard, the heavier the bill, and a mage cannot simply pour their wealth into it and forget the cost. The slots are finite and expandable only through upgrades, which caps how much any caster can keep there however rich they become. And depositing an item takes a short cast, with the item protected only once that cast completes, so a mage cut down mid-cast drops what they were trying to save.

The drain pauses while the caster is logged out. If their mana runs dry while they are in the world, the box seals: its contents stay safe, but it cannot be opened, and it holds the caster's mana recovery down until they are able to reopen it and store less. The whole spell is gated behind the mana economy of [Magic](../../../Magic.md). These limits are deliberate: they keep physical storage and the carrying decisions of [Inventory](../../../Inventory.md) relevant rather than letting one spell make them obsolete.
