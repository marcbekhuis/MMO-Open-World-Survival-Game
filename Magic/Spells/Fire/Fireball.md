# Fireball

Fireball is the fire school's heaviest blast and its siege spell: an advanced spell that asks the caster to stand in plain sight while a sphere of flame swells in their hands, then hurls it in a slow arc to burst on whatever it meets. Where Ember Bolt applies pressure and Flame Wall controls ground, Fireball settles things. It is a commitment, both for the mage who spends so much on a single throw and for everyone who watches that light build.

## Gameplay Role

The wind-up is the heart of the spell. The caster holds out an open hand and the sphere grows from a spark to a roaring, white-orange ball over several long seconds, throwing hard light across the ground, drawing a rising roar from the air, and lighting the caster's face for every enemy within sight. Taking damage or being thrown off balance breaks the channel and the sphere gutters out, so a mage needs a shield wall, a doorway, or a quiet hour before a siege to cast it at all. Once released, the ball travels in a heavy arc rather than a straight line, slow enough that a watchful target can run clear, break line of sight, or put stone between themselves and the landing point.

On impact the sphere detonates. The blast throws people and loose objects outward, scorches armour and cloth, and sets timber, thatch, and anything oil-soaked alight. The explosion also damages structures of every material, which makes Fireball the way a fire mage breaks stone: a few well-placed blasts will open a gate, crack a parapet, or collapse a weakened wall, though a sound fortification still outlasts a lone caster. How walls, doors, and roofs answer that force is set out in [Building System](../../../Building-system.md#damage-and-siege). Defenders can read the build-up long before the throw, which gives them time to scatter, to douse thatch, or to answer the mage directly.

## Upgrade Directions

Upgrades change how the blast arrives and what it does to the world, never simply how hard it hits. A siege shell compresses the sphere into a dense, dark-cored shot that drives its force into the structure it strikes, widening its reach against walls and gates at the cost of a much smaller blast radius against people. A split shot divides the sphere in flight into several smaller bursts that scatter across a wider patch of ground, which suits open battlefields and thatch rooftops but cannot break stone on its own. A delayed fuse lets the ball land and smoulder for a moment before it bursts, so the caster can drop it behind cover or into a courtyard, while attentive enemies get one last chance to run. A longer arc lifts the throw high and far, trading accuracy and a longer flight for the reach to strike over a palisade.

## Trade-Offs

Fireball costs more mana than any other fire spell, and the wind-up holds that cost for as long as the caster channels it. The light and roar give the caster away to everyone nearby, and a mage mid-cast is a target that defenders have every reason to rush. The projectile is slow and easy to dodge once it leaves the hand, so the spell punishes careless timing and rewards setups that pin enemies in place. Rain and soaked ground weaken the blast and douse the fires it starts, and water magic can smother a half-grown sphere outright. The explosion makes no distinction between friend and foe, and it will burn the caster's own palisade as readily as the enemy's; detonating too close throws the caster back and scorches them with their own fire.
