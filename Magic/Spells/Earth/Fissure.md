# Fissure

Fissure is the earth school's siege spell: an advanced spell that sends a crack running through the ground to split stone and open the footings of a wall from beneath. Where [Seismic Pulse](Seismic-pulse.md) unsettles the people standing on the ground and [Stoneguard](Stoneguard.md) hardens the caster against them, Fissure goes after what has been built on it. It is the counterpart to [Fireball](../Fire/Fireball.md), and the two divide the work of breaking a fortress between them: fire reaches every material from a distance, while earth is slower, louder, and far more at home against stone.

## Gameplay Role

Fissure is a charged, two-handed spell under the [casting rules](../../../Magic.md#casting): both hands go into the soil, the caster cannot move at all while gathering, and plain damage breaks the gathering as readily as a stagger. It is an advanced spell, unlocked by the earth school's foundation spells, [Seismic Pulse](Seismic-pulse.md) and [Stoneguard](Stoneguard.md), and it asks for a minimum Spell Power on top of the ranks in both.

The cast is a long, low, unmistakable labour. The caster kneels, drives both hands flat into the soil, and holds them there while a deep grinding builds under their palms. The ground hums for hundreds of metres, loose stones walk across it, and pale dust lifts from the grass in a thin seam. Nothing about it can be done quietly, and nothing about it can be done on the move: the caster is rooted for the whole channel, and a stagger, a knockdown, or a blow heavy enough to break concentration ends it with a dull crack and the mana gone. A mage needs a screen of friends, a trench, or a quiet night to bring it off, and a base that hears that grinding at its walls knows exactly what is coming.

When the channel completes, a fault opens at the caster's hands and races along the line they were facing, a crack as wide as a man's hand at its start and wider as it advances, running through connected solid ground until its force is spent. Where it meets a stone wall or a masonry foundation it keeps going through it, splitting courses and shearing mortar, and the damage it does is the earth damage that [Building System](../../../Building-system.md#damage-and-siege) sets out: stone fears it, packed earth gives to it, and timber and thatch, which flex, shrug most of it off. The real prize is the foundation. A cracked footing weakens every piece resting on it, so a few well-aimed casts will bring a tower down on its own base or drop a whole run of wall into the ditch long before the stone itself is worn through. The crack also tears open the ground around it, collapsing shallow tunnels and cellars, spilling rock from nearby slopes, and throwing a cracked field of broken earth across the approach that is miserable to cross.

## Upgrade Directions

Upgrades change how the crack travels and where it bites, never simply how hard it hits. Faultline narrows the fault into a long, thin seam that follows a line of wall from end to end, striking many pieces lightly rather than one heavily, which suits cutting a curtain wall loose from its towers. Undermine drives the crack down instead of out, so that it ignores the face of a wall and works at the footing beneath, at the price of a much shorter run and almost no effect on anything standing above the ground. Muffled Strain wraps the channel in a damped, deeper tone that carries only a fraction of the distance, at the cost of a still longer cast with the caster rooted that much longer. Settled Ground lets the fault carry through mud, sand, and loose scree that would otherwise swallow it, trading some reach for reliability on poor footing.

## Trade-Offs

Fissure demands more from the caster than any other earth spell, and its cast is longer than even Fireball's: the gathered mana is held for the whole channel, and the caster, rooted and kneeling, is the easiest target on the field. Every cast consumes a charged mana crystal, and a siege of many casts drains the mana level of the ground until every caster there, attacker and defender alike, recovers nothing. The spell travels only through connected solid ground, so open water, a wide ditch of loose spoil, or a floor built on stilts stops it dead, and it cannot touch anything held in the air. The crack is slow compared with a thrown ball of fire, and its line is plain to see in the lifted dust, so alert defenders can leave the seam, shore up a footing, or answer the mage before it arrives. It does not choose its side: the fault runs on past the target and will split the caster's own palisade, cellar, or camp if they stand along its line, and a base shaken repeatedly this way can bring its rockfalls down on attackers as readily as defenders. Every cast is a beacon that draws the attention of nearby creatures, and the broken ground it leaves behind scars the land for a long time, which is a cost a mage who means to settle there afterwards has to weigh.
