# Reputation System

Reputation is the memory the world's NPC factions keep of a character. Every realm, settlement, sworn order, trade combine, and outlaw town described in [Guilds and Factions](Guilds-and-factions.md) holds an opinion of each adventurer it has dealt with or heard of, and that opinion is built from one thing only: what the character has done to and for its people. It decides which gates open, what a vendor charges, who comes to a player's aid, and who comes for their head, and it ties quests, raids, trade, travel, and settlement access into one long social memory.

What players do to one another is no part of it. A killing between players beyond the guarded ground is settled by the rules of [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md) and [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md), and no faction thinks better or worse of either party for it.

## Reputation Types

Local reputation is the standing a character holds with one faction, settlement, or region. Helping a town, defending its people, completing agreements, and supporting its economy builds trust there. Stealing from its people, killing them, raiding it, and betraying it damage that relationship and invite hostility.

Global reputation is the broader pattern of a character's dealings with the world's factions. A settlement that has never met a character does not know every detail of their past, but it hears rumours, reads faction reports, and sees titles and posted bounties. A character known for defending hamlets finds trade and travel a little easier wherever they go, while one known for sacking them is warned, refused, watched, or attacked by people they have never wronged.

## What Moves Standing

Every change in standing comes from an act toward NPCs, and each act is weighed by what it cost or saved the faction concerned. At the small end stand a theft from a market stall, a fair run of trade, or a delivery made on time. Above them come the killing of a villager, a contract fulfilled, a convoy escorted home. Heavier still are the killing of guards, the breaking of a convoy on the road, the defence of a hamlet against a raid, and the materials and labour given to rebuild one. At the top stand the acts a faction remembers for years: a town raided and its workshops burnt, a siege broken, a strike at a great city. A small theft never carries the consequence of a destroyed forge, and a single helpful delivery never erases a long record of violence.

A character answers for what acts in their name. The kills of a bonded creature and the work of hired or enslaved hands count as their keeper's own.

Standing recovers slowly enough that choices matter. Time alone wears the edge off a bad name but does not clear it, and the way back is set out under [Redemption](#redemption).

## Witnesses

A faction can hold against a character only what it knows they did. An act against its people counts when one of them sees or hears it and lives to tell of it: a farmer in the next field, a guard on the wall, a carter who gets away from a broken convoy. The witness has to be one of the faction's own people. Other players and a character's own hired hands carry no tales to anyone. The moment of knowing is the end of the encounter: if any witness is alive and free when the violence stops, the act is known and standing moves at once, while a witness cut down before they get clear tells nobody.

This makes the quiet crime a real choice with a real cost. A theft nobody notices and an ambush that leaves no survivors move no standing, but leaving no survivors means killing everyone who saw, and each of those deaths is a further act that needs the same silence. On a lonely frontier track that can be done. In a hamlet it means killing the hamlet, and in a great city it cannot be done at all. The faction still feels what it lost and answers the loss itself, with the stronger patrols and closed services of [Raids and Conquest](Raids.md#npc-settlement-raids). It simply holds no name to it.

Help is seldom unseen, since it is given to someone: a contract has its giver, an escort its convoy, a defence the people defended. Once an act is known where it happened, it travels as [Reputation Types](#reputation-types) describes, far and fast for a great deed and hardly at all for a small one.

## Rivals and Allies

No faction stands alone. Each has rivals it contests ground and trade with and allies it leans on, as [Setting and Lore](Setting-and-lore.md#the-many-realms-and-the-race-outward) describes, and what a character does to one of them is felt by the others. Harm done to a faction pleases its rivals and troubles its allies, and help given to it does the reverse.

The others feel only a fraction of what the faction itself feels, and that fraction grows with the size of the deed. The death of one farmer moves nobody beyond the village that buries him. A convoy broken on a contested road is noticed by the rival that competes for that road. A raid on a city is news in every court: the realm that was struck turns on the raider in full, its rivals open their gates a good deal wider, and its allies grow cold. The gain with a rival is always smaller than the loss with the faction wronged, so playing realms against one another is a real strategy with a real price, and nobody becomes a hero in one realm simply by being a butcher in the next.

## Standing and Its Effects

Each faction places a character in one of five bands, and a character holds a separate place with every faction.

| Standing | How the faction treats the character |
|---|---|
| **Honoured** | Its best prices, its contracts of trust, its restricted districts and finest stations, guards who come to the character's aid on its ground, and safe passage through its territory |
| **Welcome** | Fair prices, the ordinary run of quests and contracts, and hired guards on good terms |
| **Unknown** | The treatment any stranger gets: services at the listed price and nothing on trust |
| **Unwelcome** | Higher prices, refused lodging and healing, no hired guards, and patrols that watch and warn |
| **Hunted** | Closed gates, guards and patrols that attack on sight, a bounty on the character's head, and raids against their base |

Being hunted by a realm closes its great cities along with everything else it holds. The character loses that realm's banks, markets, and portals and the safe place to log out that its cities offer, and what they banked there stays out of their reach until the gates open to them again. The guard's protection described in [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md#safe-cities-and-the-reach-of-the-guard) covers only those the guard tolerates, so on that realm's guarded ground any player may strike a hunted character without the guard answering. A character hunted by the realm of their own bound city can no longer wake there and binds instead at whichever settlement will still have them. The independent settlements, the outlaw towns above all, take in those the realms have cast out, with thinner markets and no guard strong enough to make them safe.

The raids a faction sends against a base are set out in [Raids and Conquest](Raids.md#player-base-raids), and what a bad name does to hired guards in [NPCs](NPCs.md#hired-guards).

## Bounties

A faction that hunts a character puts a price on them. The bounty is posted on the boards of its settlements, paid from the faction's own coffers, and open to any player who brings the character down, as well as to the NPC hunters the faction sends after them. It is paid once. Dying does not mend the standing that caused it, and the faction posts a new price only when the character gives it fresh cause. A faction's bounty is never paid to a member of the wanted character's own party, guild, or alliance.

Players post bounties on one another as well, for their own reasons and with their own coin. Those have nothing to do with standing and are set out in [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md#the-price-on-a-head).

## Enslaving NPCs

Players may enslave NPCs as labour and guards, as [NPCs](NPCs.md#enslaved-labour-and-guards) sets out, and the world remembers it harder than almost anything else a player can do. The loss is global rather than local: settlements that never met the character hear of it through rumour, faction reports, and the captives who escape, and the mark shows in the titles and bounties others can read. It is also a standing cost rather than a single blow. Taking a captive is the heaviest part, and every captive still held keeps weighing on the holder's name, so a group that runs a gang of slaves pays for each of them in the services and welcome it loses. Releasing captives lifts that weight, and doing so after the fact is the kind of visible restitution that [Redemption](#redemption) asks for, though it never wipes the record clean. Buying slaves from a slaver caravan is cheaper in standing than taking them by force, because the sin is shared with the seller, but only cheaper. The same logic marks the hired guards of [NPCs](NPCs.md#hired-guards): their loyalty follows the employer's name, so an employer with a slaver's reputation finds their guards deserting.

## Mixed Reputation

A character can be a hero in one region and a villain in another. Because every faction keeps its own account and rivals warm to what allies resent, faction tension, political intrigue, smuggling, mercenary work, and roleplay all have room to operate without every act being flattened into a single morality score. Keeping some relationships while burning others is viable, and it is costly: every friend a character makes this way comes with an enemy.

## Redemption

Reputation is a long-term resource. It governs access, prices, safety, faction support, settlement hostility, and raid risk, and it becomes part of who a character is in the world. A bad name can be mended, but only through effort the faction can see: restitution paid for what was taken or destroyed, dangerous service on its behalf, captives released, a rival's raid turned back at its gates. A hunted character begins that road outside the walls, through the faction's outlying people and the go-betweens willing to carry an offer, since the gates stay shut until the first amends have been made.
