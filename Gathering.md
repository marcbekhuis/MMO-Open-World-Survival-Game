# Gathering

Everything a player makes begins as something standing in the world: a tree, an outcrop of stone, a seam of ore in a cliff face, a stand of reeds, a deer. Gathering is the work of taking it, and in this world that work is physical. Resources are things a player can see, walk up to, and judge by eye, and they are worked with tools that strike them under the same rules as a sword strikes a shield. A heavy felling axe takes more from a trunk with each blow than a hatchet, a stone pick barely marks iron ore, and a deer brought down with a clean arrow yields a better hide than one hacked down with a hammer. This document sets out what resources are, how they are worked, how they run out and come back, and how hunting turns into hide and meat. What becomes of the material afterwards is the business of [Crafting](Crafting.md).

## Resources in the World

Resources are physical things placed in the world, in the manner of the survival games players will compare this one to. Trees stand in the forests, outcrops of stone and boulders lie across the land, ore and crystal show as seams and veins in rock faces and cave walls, and plants, reeds, fibre, berries, herbs, and mushrooms grow where their country suits them. Each one holds a store of material, and the biome decides what is there to take: timber from the [Forest](Biomes/Forest.md), ore and crystal from the [Mountain Range](Biomes/Mountain-range.md), shellfish and salvage from the [Coast](Biomes/Coast.md).

Working a resource is striking it. Each blow of an axe or a pick, and each held pull at a plant, takes material straight into the gatherer's inventory, with the weight that material carries under [Inventory](Inventory.md). What does not fit drops at the gatherer's feet as a stack or a bundle in the world. The resource shows its store running down: a trunk is cut deeper with every blow until it falls and leaves a stump, an outcrop cracks and shrinks until the last of it breaks away, a seam is followed back into the rock until it runs out. A gatherer can read a site's wealth by looking at it, and so can anyone who arrives after them.

Some things need no tool at all. Sticks, loose stones, fibre, berries, and herbs are gathered by hand, which is how a new character with nothing makes their first tools.

## Tools and Hardness

Every material has a hardness, and every tool has a hardness and a weight. A tool strikes a resource under the impact rules of [Melee Combat](Melee-combat.md#when-a-strike-lands): the energy of the blow, the edge or point that lands, and the hardness of the tool against the hardness of what it hits decide how much a blow takes. A tool harder than the material bites; a heavier tool swung well bites deeper, at a higher stamina cost per swing; a tool softer than the material barely scratches it and wears quickly doing so. A stone pick works soft rock, chalk, and copper; iron ore wants a pick of iron or better; the hardest crystals of the deep places want better still. Nothing is locked away by rule. A player can hack at iron ore with a stone pick for as long as their patience and the pick last, which is not long.

Each kind of resource has its right tool: an axe for wood, a pick for stone and ore, a sickle or a knife for plants and fibre, and a knife for butchering. The wrong tool works badly or not at all, so an axe on stone mostly ruins the axe. Tools wear by the rules of [Inventory](Inventory.md#wear-and-repair), and a dull axe or a blunted pick takes less from every blow until it is repaired.

## Skill

The gathering disciplines of [Skills](Skills.md), logging, mining, harvesting, skinning, fishing, and salvage, are practised by doing them. A practised gatherer takes more from each blow, spends less stamina on each swing, wears their tools less, and is more likely to turn up the rarer finds a resource holds, a crystal in a seam of ordinary ore or a perfect pelt from an ordinary kill. Their ranks rise without a ceiling, as every skill's do, and they are what the heavy work of a frontier settlement is built on.

## Depletion and Renewal

Living resources come back where they were taken; minerals do not. A picked plant regrows in place over a few hours of play. A felled tree leaves its stump and a sapling, and the sapling grows back into a tree over several real days, so a logged hillside is bare for a while and then is forest again. A mined-out seam or a broken outcrop is gone for good. New seams surface elsewhere in the same region over time, opened by erosion, rockfalls, and the shifting of the deep places, so the best places to mine move around the world, and finding them is part of the work. A mining camp that has stripped its valley moves on, and a prospector who knows where the new seams are showing has something worth selling. How fast plants regrow, trees grow back, and new seams surface are server settings under [Server Settings](Server-settings.md#official-and-community-servers).

## Hunting and Butchering

A dead creature is a carcass lying where it fell, and butchering it is a held action under [Actions](Actions.md#interacting-with-the-world): kneeling over it with a knife and working, for a time that grows with the size of the animal. What the carcass yields, meat, hide, bone, sinew, fangs, and whatever rarer parts the creature carries, depends on the butcher's skill, the knife, and above all on how the creature died. Damage carries through to the yield. A hide that took a dozen sword cuts comes away in pieces, a skull crushed by a hammer yields no head worth keeping, and meat around a burned or crushed wound is spoiled. A clean kill, an arrow to the heart, a single thrust, a broken neck, leaves the most behind, which makes hunting well a skill of its own and the choice of weapon part of it. The wounds that do this are the located wounds of [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md#where-a-blow-lands).

A carcass does not wait. Its meat spoils under the rules of [Survival](Survival.md#food-and-hunger), its blood draws scavengers and predators, and a large creature, a buffalo or a giant, has to be butchered in parts over a long, exposed time, so bringing down something big is the start of a problem as much as the end of one.

## Noise and Risk

Gathering is loud. The blows of an axe carry across a valley and the ring of a pick through a cave, adding to the noise of [Movement](Movement.md#noise) that creatures and players hear, and a felled tree's fall is heard further still. A lone woodcutter at the edge of the frontier is announcing themselves with every swing, which is why gathering in contested country is a group activity, with someone watching while someone works, and why the raider at a stone wall with a pickaxe in the [Building System](Building-system.md) is heard long before they are through.
