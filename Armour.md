# Armour

Armour is metal, leather, and cloth standing between a blow and the body, and it protects the way real armour does: where it covers, against what it was made to stop, and at a price in weight, breath, sight, and sound. No piece of armour makes its wearer harder to hurt everywhere at once. A breastplate turns a sword on the chest and does nothing for the thigh below it; a mail shirt stops a cut and lets a hard thrust split its rings; a great helm keeps a hammer off the skull and leaves its wearer half blind. Choosing armour is choosing which blows to fear, and fighting an armoured opponent is finding the blows they cannot stop. Creatures wear their armour as hide, scale, shell, and stone, by the same rules. This document sets out how armour meets a blow, what it costs to wear, and how it wears out. What a blow does once it passes armour is set out in [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md).

## Coverage and Gaps

Every piece of armour covers part of the body, and nothing covers all of it. A breastplate covers the chest and belly, a mail hauberk the torso and upper arms, greaves the shins, a helmet the skull and, if it is closed, the face. Between and beneath them are the gaps every armour has: the armpits, the inside of the elbow, the back of the knee, the neck below the helm, the face behind an open one. Where a blow connects is decided by the strike itself, as [Melee Combat](Melee-combat.md#when-a-strike-lands) sets out, so a blow that lands on plate meets plate and a blow that finds the armpit meets only the padding underneath.

Armour is worn in layers, and the layers add up. A padded gambeson softens blows on its own and sits under everything else; mail goes over it; plate goes over the mail where the wearer can afford it. A blow meets each layer in turn from the outside, and each takes what it can stop.

The gaps are why technique matters against armour. A thrust with its narrow point is the weapon that finds a gap, and a light blade aimed at the joints of a plated opponent does far more than one beaten against the plate. The [Combat Skills](Skills/Combat.md) that refine aim and edge alignment are worth most against the best-armoured enemies.

## Materials Against Blows

Each material meets cut, pierce, and crush in its own way, and each has a limit beyond which a blow drives through anyway.

- **Padded cloth** takes the sting out of a crushing blow and stops a weak cut, but a determined edge or point goes through it.
- **Leather** turns glancing cuts and slows a point, and is light and quiet.
- **Mail** stops cuts almost entirely, and is the reason a sword is not enough against a well-armed soldier. A hard thrust or a heavy arrow can split its rings, and a crushing blow passes through it into the body as bruising and broken bone.
- **Plate** stops cuts, turns most thrusts and arrows, and spreads a crushing blow over a wide area. It does not make its wearer immune to a war hammer: the hammer dents the plate, and the force that passes through is still enough to bruise, break, and, on a helmet, concuss.

A blow weaker than what a layer can stop glances or is stopped there, and only its blunt force reaches the body. A blow stronger than that drives through with whatever energy it has left. Better materials raise the limit, from boiled leather and iron mail to steel plate and the rare metals of the deep places, and the best armour of the late game stops blows that would go through anything a new character can make. Armour carries no stat or skill requirements. Its weight is its limit: a character who wants to wear plate needs the Carry Weight and Stamina to bear it, and a character who cannot bear it simply cannot move well in it.

## Weight, Breath, and Sound

Armour is worn weight, and it counts in full toward the burden that [Movement](Movement.md#carrying-weight) turns into slower paces, heavier starts and turns, and a lost sprint. A full harness of plate leaves little room for anything else a character might carry before they are over their limit, which is why the heavily armoured travel light in every other respect or bring a mount.

Armour is loud. Mail rings and plate clanks with every stride, adding to the noise of each gait that creatures and players hear, so an armoured column announces itself long before it arrives. Leather and padded cloth are nearly silent, which is half of why scouts and hunters wear them.

Armour also holds heat. In the [Scorching Desert](Biomes/Scorching-desert.md), a soldier in mail under a summer sun is cooking inside it, and in the [Tundra](Biomes/Tundra.md) bare metal against the skin draws the warmth out of a body; the temperature pressures of [Survival](Survival.md#temperature-and-exposure) read what a character wears as well as the weather.

## Helmets, Sight, and Hearing

The helmet decides more fights than any other piece, because a blow to the head is the most dangerous blow there is, as [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md#the-head) describes. It is also the piece that most changes how its wearer meets the world. An open helm leaves the face bare and the view and hearing clear. A visored or closed helm protects the face and the eyes, and in exchange narrows the view to what can be seen through its slits and muffles the world outside, so the faint sound of a stalking pack or the creak of a bow being drawn reaches its wearer late or not at all. In first person this is felt directly: the edges of the screen are the inside of the helm, and the sounds are the sounds inside it. A fighter in a great helm is very hard to kill in the line of battle and very easy to surprise from the side.

## Wear and Repair

Armour that takes blows is damaged by them. A dented plate, a split run of mail, or a cut strap protects less in the place it was struck until it is repaired, so a long fight wears holes in a harness exactly where it was hit hardest. Like all gear, armour has a condition and a maximum condition that wear and are repaired by the rules of [Inventory](Inventory.md#wear-and-repair). Repair is the work of the armourer's and leatherworker's stations of [Crafting](Crafting.md), and the wear that every death puts on carried equipment under [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md) falls on armour as on everything else.

## Shields

A shield is armour carried in the hand rather than worn. It covers whatever the bearer puts it in front of, which makes it the most protective piece of all when it is in the right place and no protection at all when it is not. How a shield blocks and how much of a blow it absorbs belong to the guard of [Melee Combat](Melee-combat.md#when-a-strike-lands), and its weight counts toward the burden like any other armour.

## Creatures

Creatures wear their armour as part of their bodies, with the same coverage, materials, and gaps. A boar's hide turns a glancing cut; a [Stone Giant](Creatures/Stone-giant.md)'s body is stone, which no blade will open and only crushing force will crack; a scaled hide has its overlaps and its soft underside. The gaps in a creature's natural armour are its weak points, and finding them is the knowledge the bestiary rewards: the soft throat, the joint behind the shoulder, the belly a charging animal exposes when it rears. That is how [Creatures](Creatures.md) are meant to be fought, as information rather than as health bars.
