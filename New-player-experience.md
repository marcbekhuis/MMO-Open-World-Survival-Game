# New Player Experience

A harsh, full-loot world has to be careful about how it greets a newcomer, because the same stakes that make the frontier thrilling can drive away a player who meets them in the first ten minutes with no idea what happened. The answer here is a guided, protected start that climbs steeply rather than a softened core: the early hours teach the game in safety, and the real danger arrives only when the player chooses to walk toward it. This document covers how a character is made, the order in which each core system is first taught, and what a first evening in the world looks like.

## Character Creation

A new player begins by making a character from one of the three races set out in [Player Progression](Player.md), with deep control over appearance, the face, build, hair, colouring, and the scars and wear that make a character recognisably their own, rendered at the fidelity described in [Art Direction](Art-direction.md). What they do not choose at creation is a class, because there are none. Identity is not picked from a menu but earned in play: the stats invested and the skills practised over a character's life are what turn them into a duelist, a siege-breaker, a cartographer, or a smith, so two characters of the same race can grow into entirely different roles. Creation sets the starting point and the face in the mirror; the world writes the rest.

## The First Hours

A new character wakes in or beside one of the [great cities](Structures/Great-cities.md), inside the ring of safety that the NPC guards of [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md) hold against violence. The game teaches itself in the open rather than from behind a wall of pop-ups, and it follows one rule: a system is taught at the moment it first matters, by the thing itself, at a cost small enough to forgive. The lesson is a task from the [Quest System](Quests.md) or the ordinary business of the city, the cue is something the player can see, hear, or feel in the world, and the number behind it can always be read again on demand through the interface of [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md). A new character begins with starter equipment and nothing else: no coin, no trade goods, nothing of value to anyone but its wearer. No vendor buys starter gear and no player has reason to take it, so the first lessons in fighting and dying cost nothing that matters, and every coin a character ever holds is one they earned in the world. The sequence below is the same in every starting hub, and only its surface changes with the people's city.

### The First Twenty Minutes

The first thing the world teaches is that a character owns nothing they are not holding. A newcomer begins with no storage at all, only two hands, and the first errand makes the point without a word of explanation: a quartermaster loads them with a crate of provisions for the training yard, and while both hands are full they cannot draw a weapon or open a door. A belt with two small pouches is issued at the yard, and then a modest pack worn on the back, so the lesson of [Inventory](Inventory.md#design-philosophy) arrives as a relief earned rather than a rule recited: every slot a character has comes from something they wear, and they feel their burden in the weight figure and in a slower stride as the pack fills.

The crate is carried across the district at a walk, and the walk back is where **stamina** is met. The errand is timed by a trainer waiting at the yard, which invites the player to run, and the stamina bar appears only now, draining as they sprint and refilling as they slow, as [Movement](Movement.md#stamina) describes. By the time the trainer has made them run the length of the yard once more, they have learned that effort is a budget.

**Gathering and crafting** follow in the same stretch. The yard needs bandages and the larder needs rations, and the materials grow, hang, or lie beside the city's own gates: flax and cloth, a kitchen herb, a cord of firewood. A workshop NPC shows how to turn the one into the other at a bench, the player carries the result to the person who asked for it, and the thing they made is used in front of them. That use is the lesson: the bandage they craft is the one they are handed again in the next minutes.

**Wounds** are met in the training yard. A sparring bout against a drilled opponent ends in a clean cut that bleeds, the screen edges redden, and the arm hangs a little heavier, which is the whole of [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md) in miniature. The bandage from the bench stops the bleeding, and the player learns what a wound costs and what mends it while nothing is at stake.

The **map** arrives with the errand that follows. Every character is handed the rudimentary chart that [Cartography](Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md#how-maps-grow) gives to everyone, showing the coast, the rivers, the roads, and the settlements of the city's ring, and the first quest sends the player to a hamlet on it by way of a gate, so that the first act of navigation is following a road on a map toward a place the map names. The map also marks where the player stands and which way they face, as [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md#a-baseline-for-everyone) describes, so a glance at it keeps the heading honest.

### The First Hour

The hour belongs to the farm belt, the patrolled country the road passes through as described in [Structures](Structures.md#how-settlements-spread), and the systems that need time to bite arrive here. **Hunger** is felt for the first time after hard labour. A felling or hauling errand for the hamlet is the heaviest work the player has done so far, and heavy labour burns the stomach in about an hour, so the gauge appears on the screen's edge, as [Survival](Survival.md#food-and-hunger) sets out, just as the player starts to feel it in the slower refill of their stamina. The cooked rations they made in the first twenty minutes are in their pouch, and eating them is the lesson, along with how much better a prepared meal fills than a raw one. A newcomer learns provisioning because the first time it matters, they have what it takes.

**Exposure** follows at the first dusk. The return from the hamlet crosses a ford, the player wades through wet to the waist, the evening wind picks up, and the character begins to shiver, the hands shaking and the aim wavering, which is the cue [Survival](Survival.md#temperature-and-exposure) describes. A farmstead's lit hearth is close enough to reach, and its owner waves wet travellers in without a question; nothing about the lesson is deadly, and it ends with the player standing warm at a fire, knowing what wet and wind do. The same hearth shows where a bed is, and a rested sleeper is the first hint that the **fatigue** clock of [Survival](Survival.md#fatigue-and-rest), a long one set by the length of a session, is looked after by beds and shelters.

The hamlet teaches **reputation**. The errand's recipient is an NPC who remembers the player, greets them by what they did, and sells to them at a better price on the next visit, which is the first time the player sees the local reputation of [Reputation System](Reputation-system.md#reputation-types) turn into something concrete. The same hamlet gives the next errand, a short survey of its fields and roads that sends the player off the road to explore, and every piece of new ground charted fills their map and gives the first experience in [Cartography](Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md#levelling-the-skill), so the first hour ends with a map that is slightly more theirs than it was.

### Before the Frontier

The stake of the whole game, **full loot**, is not left to be discovered the hard way. The road out of the farm belt ends at the last watchtower, and the guards who hold it speak plainly to anyone who stops there: past this point the patrols do not go, and what a player carries is what they can lose. The edge is made readable in the world, as [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md#the-edge-of-safety) sets out, and the player's first trip beyond it is an errand set up to teach the rule while the cost is small. A small band of bandits ambushes them on the road, and the loss, whether the player yields, runs, or fights and falls, is a satchel of starter gear that can be recovered from the band's camp. The whole of that lesson is in [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md#the-first-full-loot-encounter).

That ambush also teaches the **corpse run**. A player who dies there sees the marker that [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md#the-corpse-marker) pins on their map, walks back to the body inside the recovery window of [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md#dying-to-the-world), and finds it stripped, with the quest log pointing at the camp. They have lost something real, they know exactly how, and they know the way back. Banking is taught beside it: the city's bank and the guard's advice point out that wealth in storage is safe and wealth on the body is not, and a player who ends the hour with something worth keeping learns to leave it behind.

Nothing in this sequence is a lock. A player who ignores every errand can walk out to the last tower and across it, and the rules they meet there are the real ones; what the sequence guarantees is that anyone who follows it meets each rule in the order that lets them survive it.

### The Same Sequence in Each Hub

The sequence above is told in a human great city, with its militia yard, workshops, bazaar, and road gates, and the other two peoples teach the same beats through their own [starting hubs](Structures/Great-cities.md#starting-hubs). An elven newcomer is loaded with the crate and sent along canopy paths, so the lesson of two full hands is also a lesson in balance, stamina is spent on climbing and crossing rope bridges, gathering happens in the root-level gardens, and the first dusk and its cold are met in the open air among the branches, with the way out leading through protected groves toward the deeper forest. A dwarven newcomer hauls the crate down from the ore lifts, learns stamina in the training pits, gathers ore and cuts stone for the first bench, and meets hunger sooner because mining is heavy labour; the tunnel gates lead out to exposed mountain passes, where the cold arrives before the dusk does. In all three, the last watchtower of the road, or its equivalent at the tunnel gate and the grove's edge, is where the stake of the frontier is spoken aloud.

## A First Evening

The human city lies behind a newcomer by the time the light begins to go amber. They woke that morning with nothing in their hands, and carried a crate of provisions across the militia yard with their arms full and their sword on the rack, and felt a fool for it until a belt and a pack were buckled on, and they felt every kilogram of the first loaf of bread they put in the pouch. By afternoon they have run the length of the yard until their breath gave out, learned to wrap a cut they took from a drilled opponent's blunted blade, and taken the road through the gate with a map in the pack and a hamlet's name on it.

The road runs through farms, and farms give way to fewer farms. A woman in a field straightens, looks at the newcomer's pack and the hatchet on their belt, and points to a stack of cord that wants splitting before the weather turns. It takes an hour. By the end the stomach is a hollow, the arms burn, and a small icon of a loaf has appeared at the edge of sight, and the player sits on a stump to eat what they cooked that morning, and finds it fills them better than anything they have eaten in the world so far. The woman remembers their face when they pass her gate on the way back, and nods.

The ford comes at dusk. The water is up from the afternoon's rain and the crossing is wide, and they come out of it wet to the ribs into a wind that has begun to find the gaps in their clothes. Within a few hundred paces their hands have started to shake. Smoke from a farmhouse chimney lies low across the field, and a man at the gate waves them toward his hearth without asking a question. They stand at the fire until the shaking stops, and when they look out of the window the road is a pale line in the dusk and the country on either side of it has gone dark.

They walk on, because the map shows a last tower and they are curious. It stands where the fields end, a squat watchtower of grey stone with a lit lamp at the top and a guard on the road, a woman with a spear, who has watched the road empty for hours. She asks where the newcomer is bound, and when they point beyond the lamp she says only that nothing past the tower answers to the city, and that whatever they carry out there is what they lose. Somewhere beyond the lamp, far off in the black, a wolf starts and a second answers. The newcomer stands there a long moment with the cold coming back into their hands, looking at where the road goes on without a guard, and then turns toward the city, where the lights are, and sleeps that night in a borrowed bed with the road still in their head.

## Crossing into the Frontier

The safety is real, but it has an edge, and crossing it is the player's own decision. Travel outward and the patrols thin, the guarantees fall away, and the full-loot frontier of [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md) opens up, exactly as steeply as the design intends. Nothing forces a player across that line before they are ready, and everything rewards them for choosing it when they are. This is the sharp climb: the game never pretends the frontier is gentle, it simply makes sure a player knows the rules, and has something worth protecting, before the frontier gets to test them. The first law of the wilds, never carry what you cannot afford to lose, lands far harder on a player who has already learned, in safety, what it means to lose.
