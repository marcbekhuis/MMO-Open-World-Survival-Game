# Raids and Conquest

A raid is the moment the world's walls are tested. Players raid NPC settlements, NPC factions raid each other and player bases, and players raid one another's homes, whether their owners are awake or asleep. Raids are where building, reputation, territory, and loss meet, and their outcomes are what make the frontier's history: a burnt district, a fallen keep, a guild driven off its ground.

## NPC Settlement Raids

Settlements are dynamic targets for both player and NPC aggression, creating emergent world events that connect combat, reputation, trade, and rebuilding.

When players raid an NPC settlement, the objective is usually to steal resources, damage infrastructure, free prisoners, seize a strategic position, or weaken a hostile faction. The reward is limited by what the raiders can carry away and by whether they survive the escape. The cost is social as much as mechanical: the affected faction may reduce reputation, post bounties, close services, strengthen patrols, or retaliate against player bases.

NPC factions can also raid settlements because of war, hunger, territorial pressure, revenge, or resource scarcity. Damage to a settlement cuts into its production, trade, guards, and services for a time, which gives nearby players a reason to intervene. Friendly players can join the defence and earn reputation, rewards, and local trust by protecting key NPCs, gates, workshops, or supply stores.

Recovery after a raid takes time. Damaged services such as blacksmiths, traders, and storage close until they are repaired, and players can speed the rebuilding through quests, donated materials, escort work, and defence against follow-up attacks. The outcome shifts faction attitudes and leaves its mark on the region's history.

## Player Base Raids

Player-built bases are always part of the risk landscape, especially in open or contested zones. PvP raids can focus on stealing loot, damaging structures, weakening defenders, or forcing a rival group to relocate. They are the organised end of the open conflict described in [Conflict and PvP](Conflict-and-pvp.md). A raid can come through the wall, over it, or from the sky. Axes, picks, rams, siege engines, fire, and destructive magic break pieces down at speeds set by their material, ladders and grappling lines go over the top, and flyers land raiders inside the yard, as the [Building System](Building-system.md#damage-and-siege) and [Flight](Flight.md#the-sky-in-conflict) describe. Destroying a base's claim marker is often the turning point, since it lets attackers build inside the walls and starts the base decaying.

There is no offline protection. A base can be raided whether its owners are online or not, and the goods banked inside it, safe as they are from a death in the field under [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md), are only as safe as the base itself. What protects an absent group is what they built and whom they know: a base designed so that breaking in costs more time than a raiding party has, watch systems that rouse defenders, alliances that answer a call, storage hidden where nobody thinks to dig, terrain that funnels attackers into a killing ground, and escape routes for the goods and the people. Players who sleep inside their own walls are part of what a raid can find, since a character stays in the world when its player logs off, and so are the tamed creatures in their shelters ([Taming and Control](Taming-and-control.md#bonds-while-away)). Even a base on the back of a [Flying Leviathan](Creatures/Flying-leviathan.md) can be raided while its keeper is away, though the leviathan has its own defence, set out in [Flight](Flight.md#mana-borne-flight).

NPC faction raids can target player bases when reputation, location, wealth, or previous aggression makes the base a threat. Low reputation brings more frequent and fiercer raids, and a rich base draws more organised assaults. Defenders need to adapt to both human attackers and NPC forces, because each creates different pressure.

## Strategic Implications

Raids keep territory alive without making every structure disposable. Base placement, chokepoints, elevation, concealment, supply access, and local reputation all shape risk. A raid outcome can change regional power, interrupt the trade routes of [Economy and Trade](Economy.md), create revenge quests, and become part of the history players tell about the world.
