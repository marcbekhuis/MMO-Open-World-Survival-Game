# Prototype Findings

This is a working file, not part of the design proper. It collects what the companion Unreal Engine 5.8 prototype, `MmoSurvivalGame`, has learned by building the design: places where an idea did not survive play, needed a number the documents do not give, or turned out to work better another way. The prototype reads this document as canon and never edits the design files on its own; instead, every session in it records its findings here, and the findings move into the design documents and their `(Technical)` companions once the user accepts them. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for how this file fits the wider workflow, and [Design-backlog.md](Design-backlog.md) for questions the design itself still has to answer.

## How entries work

Each finding sits under the system it concerns, newest last. An entry names what the prototype tried, what play or testing showed, and the change it proposes, with links to the documents that change would touch. The date is the day the prototype established the finding, and the code path points at the prototype module where it can be seen. An entry stays here until its change has been written into the design or the user has rejected it; then it is removed, so this file holds only what is still waiting for a decision.

Modelling and content-creation sessions, including future ChatGPT and Codex sessions, may also add feedback here when the source design lacks information needed to produce the intended result, or when existing context is difficult to find. Name the missing or overlooked context, the assumption made, its observed consequence, and the document or link that would address it. Distinguish a missing design decision from an implementation mistake or a failure to read existing guidance. Such feedback remains a proposal until accepted; it does not authorise changes to the design documents themselves.

## Creatures

Findings from the prototype's shared creature module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Creatures/`, which the wolf now builds on.

**Creature actions are gameplay tags, and the species data holds the body, senses and attacks (1 November 2026).** The prototype moved the wolf's body onto a shared creature base and gave it a species data asset, named after the `UCreatureSpeciesData` of [Taming and Control (Technical)](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#21-species-data>). Section 7.1 of that guide sketches the replicated action as `FCreatureActionState { uint8 Action; uint8 Sequence; }` over an `ECreatureAction` enum. The prototype replicates a gameplay tag under `Creature.Action` (`Creature.Action.Tell`, `.Lunge`, `.Bite`, `.Howl`, `.Hit.Front` and so on) with the same sequence counter, and each species maps the tags it uses to montages in its data. A new species then adds actions in data instead of growing one enum shared by every creature. On a dedicated server at 150 milliseconds the client received every tell, lunge, bite and howl of the wolf encounter by tag. The species asset also proved the natural home for what sections 7.3 and 7.6 already call species data: sight radius and half-angle, hearing range and sense memory, threat memory, fear settle rate and fear sensitivity, follower and leader health and the leader's size, and one attack shape per striking action (a sphere's radius and its reach in front of the body). The attack shapes are a list of rows that each name their action, not a map keyed by the action, because the server's by-name tuning overrides cannot address map entries in Unreal Engine 5.8. The prototype then moved the brain's shared parts onto the same asset: the hesitate, recover and flee thresholds of [section 7.3](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#73-perception-and-fear>), how far a hesitating creature backs out past a fire's edge, and how the brain asks for paths. Proposed change: in [section 7.1](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#71-server-only-brains>) make the action field an `FGameplayTag`, and in [section 2.1](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#21-species-data>) list the body, leader, perception, awareness, fear, brain movement and attack-shape groups beside the taming data.

**Team filtering made wolves notice a player faster (1 November 2026).** The prototype gave every body a team and set each creature's senses to detect only teams it is hostile to, as [section 8.2](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#82-server-budgets>) of the taming guide asks. Teams are gameplay tags with a one-way table of hostile pairs in configuration, so the wolf is hostile to players without players needing an attitude towards wolves, and a body with no team is neutral to everyone. On a dedicated server with fourteen wolves in the sandbox, a pack spawned 20 metres from a standing player noticed it 0,2 to 0,3 seconds after spawning in seven runs, against 1,3 to 3,0 seconds before, when every wolf's sight also tracked every other wolf. The guide's note that the saving has not been profiled can take this as a first, indirect measure: the time-sliced sight checks reach the prey sooner when they no longer spend themselves on packmates. One caveat for the guide: the engine caches a creature's team when its senses register, so the taming bond that changes a creature's side at runtime must refresh both its senses and its registration as a seen target.

**A plain deer as test prey, and what a wolf hunt of a herd showed (1 November 2026).** The design has no ordinary deer; [Ancient Deer](Creatures/Ancient-deer.md) is a magical tundra species. The prototype added a plain deer herd as test prey (module `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Creatures/Deer/`): six deer that graze behind a leader, stand alert facing a threat within 25 metres and flee as one within 15 metres or when any deer is hurt, with a sprint faster than a wolf's (11 against 9,5 metres a second) that lasts 5 seconds instead of 8. Wolves treat deer as prey like players. In two dedicated-server hunts the pack ran the herd until its deer were winded, switched to a winded deer as the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) target rules intend, knocked down two or three and killed one or two. A first rule that let any deer strike a wolf within 2,5 metres made the herd stand and fight: 42 antler strikes in 40 seconds, a dead wolf and a broken pack, so the prototype now lets only a winded deer, which can no longer outrun the pack, turn and strike; even so, winded deer killed a wolf in one hunt and broke the pack's nerve in the other. Proposed change: decide whether the world needs a plain deer (or another common grazer) as the ordinary prey of [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) packs and players, and if so add it under Creatures with the herd, flight and cornered-strike behaviour above; the [Ancient Deer](Creatures/Ancient-deer.md) entry can take the same rule that a deer fights only when it can no longer flee.

**A brain's path requests need a back-off after failures (1 November 2026).** On a dedicated server the navigation mesh is built after the server starts, which took between 20 and 112 seconds in the prototype's sandbox, and every path request made before it is ready fails at once. The wolf brain asked again on its next tick, so the nine wolves of the two resting packs made about 1500 to 1850 failed requests in a two-minute run, most of all the requests the server handled. Waiting a quarter of a second after a failure and doubling the wait per failure in a row, up to 2 seconds, cut that to 50 to 90 failed requests and roughly halved all requests, without changing how the pack hunted. A distance-scaled re-goal with a cooldown between requests, the obvious throttle, saved nothing on top, even against a jogging target. Proposed change: [section 7.5](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#75-navigation-by-body-size>) and the scale budget of [section 8.2](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#82-server-budgets>) gain the rule that a creature backs off its path requests after failures, and that a region server's creatures expect failed requests until its navigation data is built or loaded.

## Populations and Spawning

Findings from the prototype's population module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Population/`, which brings creature groups into the sandbox through spawn zones and puts them to sleep away from players.

**Sleeping groups must be woken only by players and by groups a player woke (2 November 2026).** [Populations and Spawning](Populations-and-spawning.md#every-creature-is-someone) lets an awake creature wake a group it could sense, so that a pack drawn out by a hunter can still find the herd in the next valley. Taken literally, that rule keeps a herd and a pack awake for good once a player has woken either: each is an awake creature that can sense the other. In a dense region the waking also spreads from group to group. The prototype therefore wakes groups in two passes: first every group within its wake distance of a player, then every group within its wake distance of a hostile group that a player woke, and no further. A sleeping creature also stops counting as a threat that keeps another group awake; before that change a herd placed within sight of a sleeping pack never slept again. On a dedicated server at 150 milliseconds, a player jogging at 3,5 metres a second woke two herds at 89,8 and 87,8 metres (their senses plus a 30 metre margin, checked once a second), and their pack one step later through the herds. Both herds slept again while the player stood 300 metres away, and the whole region was asleep once the player had left. Proposed change: in [Every Creature Is Someone](Populations-and-spawning.md#every-creature-is-someone), say that only a group a player has woken can wake another, and only one step deep. In the perceiver index of [Server Architecture (Technical)](<Server-architecture (Technical).md#62-visibility-overlap-detection>), count only awake creatures as perceivers.

**A zone's memory of its kill sites must outlast its quiet period (2 November 2026).** The prototype first kept a zone's recent deaths for exactly its quiet period. A zone becomes due for new arrivals once it has been below its minimum for that same period, so the deaths that emptied it were forgotten at the moment it became due: in the first gate run a new herd appeared 7 metres from where the last one had been killed. With a separate memory of five minutes against a one-minute quiet period, the next run placed both new herds 22 metres or more from every death. The design text now says the memory outlasts the quiet period. Proposed change: give the kill-site memory its own server setting beside the quiet period in [Load and Server Settings](Populations-and-spawning.md#load-and-server-settings), and say there that it must be the longer of the two.

## Health and Wounds

Findings from the prototype's vitals module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Vitals/`, which gives every player and creature body one health and wound model.

**A data model for the missing health technical guide (5 November 2026).** The [Design Backlog](Design-backlog.md) lists health, wounds and armour among the foundation systems without a technical guide, so the prototype had to choose one. Each body plan carries a list of regions, and each region is a box in the body's own space with a role (head, torso, arm or leg), a health cost per joule, its natural armour and its wound thresholds per kind of blow. A hit arrives as energy of a kind at a contact point, finds its region by that point, meets the armour layers from the outside in, and then costs health and leaves a wound. The regions are boxes rather than the skeleton because a dedicated server does not move the bones of a body nobody renders, the same reason the creature attack shapes already avoid bones. Wounds are one record per region and kind: a second light cut on the same arm makes the cut serious instead of adding a second wound, so the record stays small and the effects of [Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md#wounds) read from at most one wound per region and kind. Injury reaches movement as one named source among several (injury, load, survival, stats) that the movement component merges, with scales multiplying and gait caps taking the lowest, so no system overwrites another's effect. Proposed change: write a *Health and Wounds (Technical)* companion from this model, with the region boxes, the wound record and the condition sources, and clear the matching line of the backlog entry.

**Numbers for the head, bleeding and falls (5 November 2026).** [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md) gives times and comparisons but no numbers. The prototype costs a joule on the head 1,5 health against 0,5 on the torso, so a head blow costs three times as much, as [The Head](Health-and-wounds.md#the-head) asks; it dazes for 0,05 seconds per joule up to 6 seconds and kills outright at 150 joules. A serious bleed costs 1,2 health a second, so it empties a person's 100 health in about 80 seconds, and a light one 0,4 a second, about four minutes, which matches the minute or two and the few minutes of [Bleeding](Health-and-wounds.md#bleeding). A fall delivers 25 joules of crush to each leg per metre beyond 4 metres. In networked runs, drops measured at 7,1 to 7,4 metres fractured both legs and left the body limping at 0,9 metres a second, and drops of 13,1 to 13,5 metres killed, which matches the four and twelve metres of [Jumping, Crouching, and Falling](Movement.md#jumping-crouching-and-falling). Proposed change: write these numbers into [The Head](Health-and-wounds.md#the-head), [Bleeding](Health-and-wounds.md#bleeding) and the falling paragraph of [Movement](Movement.md#jumping-crouching-and-falling) as server defaults.

**Creatures stop a light bleed on their own (5 November 2026).** [Bleeding](Health-and-wounds.md#bleeding) says a light bleed left alone kills within a few minutes, and creatures cannot bind a wound. Taken literally, every wolf grazed by a spear and every deer nipped by a wolf would bleed to death minutes later, far from anyone, which also contradicts the wolf that is driven off and returns in [Creatures](Health-and-wounds.md#creatures). The prototype lets a person's scratch clot after 20 seconds, and a creature's light bleed too, while serious and grave bleeds run until treated. In a networked wolf hunt of a deer herd, two deer still died of bleeding, because repeated bites raised their wounds to serious. Proposed change: say in [Creatures](Health-and-wounds.md#creatures) that a creature's light bleed stops on its own, so only deep wounds hunt a creature down.

**The armour layer is the first column of the impact table, and it must be continuous (5 November 2026).** [Section 7 of Melee Combat (Technical)](<Melee-combat (Technical).md#7-impact-resolution>) plans an impact table per material. Ahead of the strike simulation, the prototype keeps per material only the energy of each kind a layer stops, the share of a stopped blow that goes on as bruising, and the wear per joule absorbed. Its first draft followed the table's shape, with a blow either stopped or carried through, and a review showed that it then hurt less to be hit harder: on mail a 120 joule cut passed 72 joules of crush and a 121 joule one only 1. The prototype therefore lets each layer take up to its stop energy of each kind, pass its blunt share of what it took as crush, and let the rest through, and a test checks that no material delivers less for a harder blow. In networked runs a wolf's 40 joule lunge cost a person 14 health on a bare arm and 6,7 in a mail hauberk, and the mail took a little less each lunge as it wore. Proposed change: in [section 7](<Melee-combat (Technical).md#7-impact-resolution>), make the stop and carry-through outcome a continuous function of energy and name the material rows the prototype uses (padded, leather, mail, plate and the hides), and in [Materials Against Blows](Armour.md#materials-against-blows) note that mail passes four tenths of a stopped blow as bruising.

**Wolves strike the arms, and armour gaps need hit volumes (5 November 2026).** Every wolf lunge that landed in the networked encounter struck an arm, never the torso: the pack comes in from the flanks, so the point where a lunge meets a standing body is on its side. That makes arm cover matter more against wolves than [Coverage and Gaps](Armour.md#coverage-and-gaps) suggests, where a breastplate reads as the first piece a traveller would buy. The prototype's regions also cannot express the gaps that section describes, the armpit and the back of the knee, since a region is either covered or not; that needs the swept hit volumes of [Hit Detection](<Melee-combat (Technical).md#6-hit-detection>). Proposed change: mention in [Coverage and Gaps](Armour.md#coverage-and-gaps) or the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) entry that a pack's flanking attacks land on the arms, and in the technical guide give armour pieces gap volumes finer than a region once hit volumes exist.

## Movement

Findings from the prototype's movement module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Movement/`, which every walking body builds on.

**A vault needs a base time, and traversal clips must be cut to fit (3 November 2026).** [Vaulting and Mantling](Movement.md#vaulting-and-mantling) gives a vault half a second for every metre of height, 0,30 seconds for a 60-centimetre wall and 0,55 seconds for one at waist height. In play that vault felt too fast: the body was over a knee-high wall before the crossing read as a vault, and the motion-captured vault clip of 1,60 seconds, about a third of it a run-in before the hand reaches the top, played at 2,9 to 5,3 times its authored speed. The prototype now gives a vault a base time of 0,35 seconds plus the half second a metre, 0,65 seconds at 60 centimetres and 0,9 at waist height, and plays the clip only from the hand reaching the wall to the landing, which runs at 1,1 to 1,5 times its speed. The vault still leaves the wall with four fifths of its speed, so a sprint keeps its pace; the crossing itself is slower. The mantle clips last 1,97 and 2,90 seconds and climb exactly 1 and 2 metres, so a 1,50-metre ledge plays the 2-metre climb at up to 2,5 times its speed, a climb half a metre higher than the edge, and no clip fits a climb-over, which borrows the waist-high vault. Proposed change: give the vault a base time in [Vaulting and Mantling](Movement.md#vaulting-and-mantling), as the mantle already has, and have [section 10 of Movement (Technical)](<Movement (Technical).md#10-animation-and-first-person-presentation>) require traversal animation authored or cut to the traversal times: a vault from hand plant to landing, mantles in height steps of about half a metre, and hands placed on the edge by IK or motion warping rather than by the clip.

**Onlookers need the traversal clip and its duration, not only the movement mode (3 November 2026).** A traversal reaches other clients only as a movement mode in the prototype. That was enough for one clip played at its own pace, but not for a clip chosen by kind and height and timed to a duration that onlookers cannot work out without the measured obstacle. The prototype adds two fields to the view struct that [section 8](<Movement (Technical).md#8-creatures-server-driven-bodies>) already replicates to everyone but the owner: the clip's entry in the body's list of traversal clips (one byte) and the traversal's duration in milliseconds (two bytes). A mantle's clip also plays at two rates, so that its climb ends when the capsule reaches the top of its rise. In networked runs at 150 milliseconds of latency, an onlooking client played the same clip, duration and rates as the owner and the server for both traversals it was close enough to see. Proposed change: list the two fields with the view struct in section 8, and describe the clip choice and the two-rate timing in section 10.

**A change of the movement conditions costs a burst of corrections (5 November 2026).** [Section 3 of Movement (Technical)](<Movement (Technical).md#3-intent-state-and-conditions>) stamps the conditions with the server move time, so a misprediction stays confined to the moves in flight. The prototype now changes them from injury, not only from rare inventory events: a serious leg cut takes the sprint and a fracture leaves the walk. In networked runs at 150 milliseconds, each such change while the body moved cost the owning client two or three correction lines, as many as a knockdown, and none while it stood. A fight that wounds a running player therefore snaps them back a little at each wound. Proposed change: say in section 3 that conditions which change in combat, such as injury caps, cost a correction per change unless the change is carried in the move, as the knockdown should be, and list injury among the systems that write them.

## Building and Source Art

Findings from the prototype's building module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Building/`, its model generators in `SourceArt/Building/`, and its content importers in `Scripts/Building/`.

**A timber kit needs a visual reference beyond the word photorealistic (5 November 2026).** The modelling session produced ten planked timber pieces, but chose their precise colour, finish, proportions and amount of wear without an approved reference for this kit. [Shape Language and Material Discipline](Art-direction.md#shape-language-and-material-discipline) already calls for square-hewn beams, pegged joints, visible tool marks and repairs; that guidance was available and needed closer application, rather than replacement with more generic realism language. The missing detail was which expression of timber the requested kit represents: freshly worked frontier lumber, weathered rough timber, or the planked and treated work distinguished in [Materials](Building-system.md#materials). Proposed change: add a linked timber art brief beneath [Art Direction](Art-direction.md), with an approved assembled example, front and rear views of representative pieces, and close views of grain, end grain, joints and wear. Specify the intended timber colour range, plank widths and irregularity, edge treatment, fastening method, exposed framing, and permitted gaps. References to other survival builders in [Building System](Building-system.md) establish placement behaviour; the brief must identify any visual traits being borrowed separately. The generated kit is a candidate for review, not an approved reference merely because it exists.

**New piece families need an explicit dimensional and interface brief (5 November 2026).** The prototype's existing source-art contract supplied the 3-metre module and the foundation, wall, ceiling and pillar dimensions. It did not supply the new door and window openings, hinge placement, stair profile, roof pitch or horizontal beam dimensions. The session chose a doorway 1,20 metres wide and 2,28 metres high, a matching door with 0,01 metres of clearance per edge, a window opening 1,20 metres square, fifteen stair rises and runs of 0,20 metres, and a roof rising 1,50 metres over a 3-metre run. These are authoring choices, not accepted gameplay dimensions; the steep stair has not been tested for movement feel or headroom. Proposed change: extend [Piece Definitions](<Building-system (Technical).md#22-piece-definitions>) and [Snap Compatibility](<Building-system (Technical).md#31-snap-compatibility>) with a linked kit specification covering nominal dimensions, pivots, local axes, mating planes, openings, door swing and clearances, stair landings and headroom, roof edges and ridge joins, and collision openings. Keep the executable dimensions in the prototype's source-art contract and generators, and identify which values are approved versus awaiting a prototype decision rather than treating this session's choices as canon.

**Triangle counts need a quality floor and a measured scene budget (5 November 2026).** The ten models contain 204 to 3660 source triangles each, use one material slot per mesh and share one material, and passed import with Nanite enabled. The generator's ceiling of 12000 triangles per piece was chosen during this session; neither that ceiling nor the actual counts were established by a dense-settlement performance test. Low counts and successful import therefore demonstrate neither the requested first-person visual quality nor performance at settlement scale. [Photorealistic Foundation](Art-direction.md#photorealistic-foundation) names the rendering direction, and [One Actor per Base](<Building-system (Technical).md#101-one-actor-per-base>) describes instanced pieces, but a modeller still needs an asset-class budget. Proposed change: specify review distance and required silhouette and surface detail alongside provisional geometry ranges, material-slot limits, texture memory, collision complexity, target hardware and frame-time allowances in the building technical guide. Validate those allowances against a stated number of visible building pieces and the [Hotspot Workloads and Degradation](Server-settings.md#hotspot-workloads-and-degradation) scenarios before adopting them. Do not make this kit's triangle counts a universal target or use a low count to justify visibly simple construction.

**Material delivery needs a shared surface standard and a close-view acceptance example (5 November 2026).** The session supplied a shared 2048 by 4096 texture atlas at a nominal 512 texels per metre, with base-colour, tangent-space normal and roughness maps; these were authoring choices rather than an approved surface budget. The user explicitly requested textures and normal maps, although [Photorealistic Foundation](Art-direction.md#photorealistic-foundation) already requires physically based materials. What remained unspecified was the expected delivery and appearance at arm's length: acceptable texture reuse, visible repetition, grain scale and orientation, end-grain treatment, variation between boards, and the amount of wear belonging to the base material rather than later weathering. Proposed change: link a surface-authoring standard from [Coherence at Scale](Art-direction.md#coherence-at-scale) and [Rendering, Replication, and Persistence](<Building-system (Technical).md#10-rendering-replication-and-persistence>), defining required maps, colour-space and normal conventions, texel density, texture-resolution and memory limits, and atlas or tiling policy. Include an approved close-view example under neutral and in-game lighting. Keep exact importer settings and engine-specific troubleshooting in the prototype repository.

**Whole-piece bounds do not prove that a modular piece is complete (5 November 2026).** The door frame passed its overall bounds and closed-mesh checks while the infill on both sides of the opening stopped 0,24 metres above the ground. This was a modelling error: the generator reused the solid wall's bottom-rail offset after removing that rail for the doorway. The user spotted the gaps in the delivered preview, and the corrected model now reaches the floor on both sides while leaving the opening clear. The intended behaviour did not require a new design decision. What the process lacked was a shared visual acceptance case fine enough to catch it. Proposed change: add asset-review cases to [Design Validation Cases](<Building-system (Technical).md#12-design-validation-cases>) for front and rear ground contact, unobstructed openings, matching door and frame, stair access to the next floor, and assembled wall, floor, corner and roof junctions. Review an assembled example at first-person height as well as isolated catalogue renders; explicitly check gaps, intersecting faces, texture repetition and collision agreement. The specific regression check belongs in the local generator.

**A modelling task needs a direct reading route and an explicit delivery boundary (5 November 2026).** The session found the machine paths and the prototype's source-art rules, so those were not missing. It reached the building design and technical guide directly, but did not bring the full [Art Direction](Art-direction.md) into the modelling brief early enough. The ten resulting assets also span two delivery states: the existing wall, pillar and ceiling retain gameplay registration, while seven new meshes are imported art assets without their new placement rules or interaction. Proposed change: add a short asset-authoring route to [README](README.md) and [Art Direction](Art-direction.md), linking the global art rules, the relevant system and technical guide, the chosen material or cultural brief, and the prototype's source-art contract through its recorded local checkout. A building brief then declares the required piece list, visual variants and review references, and whether delivery means source models, textured engine assets, or playable registered pieces with snapping, collision and interaction. Each hand-off records unresolved assumptions and links its review images and validation results, so the next session need not reconstruct the brief from chat history.
