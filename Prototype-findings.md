# Prototype Findings

This is a working file, not part of the design proper. It collects what the companion Unreal Engine 5.8 prototype, `MmoSurvivalGame`, has learned by building the design: places where an idea did not survive play, needed a number the documents do not give, or turned out to work better another way. The prototype reads this document as canon and never edits the design files on its own; instead, every session in it records its findings here, and the findings move into the design documents and their `(Technical)` companions once the user accepts them. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for how this file fits the wider workflow, and [Design-backlog.md](Design-backlog.md) for questions the design itself still has to answer.

## How entries work

Each finding sits under the system it concerns, newest last. An entry names what the prototype tried, what play or testing showed, and the change it proposes, with links to the documents that change would touch. The date is the day the prototype established the finding, and the code path points at the prototype module where it can be seen. An entry stays here until its change has been written into the design or the user has rejected it; then it is removed, so this file holds only what is still waiting for a decision.

## Building System

These findings come from the building prototype's first slice, the core loop of placement, snapping, stability, and collapse, built against [Building System](Building-system.md) and [Building System (Technical)](<Building-system (Technical).md>). Prototype module: `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Building/`.

**Every foundation touches the ground (11 October 2026).** The worked example in *A Wall Raised Step by Step* lets a second foundation hang over falling ground, held by its neighbour until a pillar beneath it reaches the terrain, and the technical guide's ground rule (section 3.3) allows the same. In play this produced foundations floating off ledges and made the ground skirt meaningless. The prototype now refuses any foundation that does not find the ground under all four corners and its centre within its skirt, so every foundation is a stability source; the joint that let a foundation rest on a pillar top is gone. The proposal is to rewrite the worked example around a foundation that steps down the slope on its own skirt, and to drop the held-by-a-neighbour case from the technical guide and its validation table.

**A pillar is a source only on terrain (11 October 2026).** The technical guide makes a pillar a ground source when its foot rests on terrain or on a foundation. A pillar standing on a foundation, or on a ceiling, could then restart stability at full strength halfway up a tower, which lets a builder stack storeys without limit. In the prototype a pillar is a source only when its foot is on the terrain; anywhere else its foot is an ordinary vertical joint. The proposal changes the pillar rule in the technical guide, section 3.3, to match.

**Snap compatibility belongs to the pair, not the point (11 October 2026).** The technical guide gives every snap point an `Accepts` list and a joint kind of its own. Building the kit showed that the joint kind depends on what meets what: the top of a wall is a vertical joint for a wall stacked on it and a lateral one for a ceiling laid across it. The prototype holds a single table of pair rules in the piece registry, each naming two snap types and the joint they form, matched hierarchically through the gameplay-tag tree so a handful of rules covers the kit. The proposal replaces the per-point `Accepts` and joint fields in the technical guide's data model with that pair table.

**Snap points are not occupied (11 October 2026).** Tracking which snap points are taken proved unnecessary. The prototype lets a snap point accept any number of links and prevents double placement by testing the new piece's shrunken core box for overlap with its neighbours, which also catches a second ceiling slid into the same span from another edge. The technical guide can drop snap occupancy from its records in favour of the overlap test.

**The worked example's numbers (11 October 2026).** With dressed stone losing 20 stability over a vertical joint and 350 over a lateral one, rough timber losing 60 and 250, and the bands set at blue from 900, green from 600, yellow from 350, and red below, the example reproduces as written in unit tests and over the network: stone foundations at 1000, the wall at 980, the first timber span at 730 in green, the second at 480 in yellow, and the outer span back to 750 in green once the far pillar lands. A pillar under a ceiling forms a lateral joint, which is what returns that span to green rather than blue. The proposal records these values in the technical guide as the reference tuning for its validation case.

**Raising a free-standing foundation (11 October 2026).** On flat ground every foundation sat flush, which left no way to lift a floor off wet ground or level a terrace. The prototype lets a free-standing foundation rise up to 1 metre above flush in 10-centimetre steps, inside its ground skirt so it still touches the ground and stays a source; anything snapped to it inherits the height through the snap. The proposal adds the raise to the placement rules in [Building System](Building-system.md) and its range to the technical guide.

**Snap points must be visible outside the piece (11 October 2026).** Markers drawn at the snap point itself sat inside the pillar and could not be told apart, so a builder could not choose between a wall meeting a post in its middle or flush at either corner. The prototype draws each marker just outside the piece's surface as a small arrow along the joint, colours the three pillar-face positions differently, and names the highlighted point on the screen. The proposal adds this to the build-mode presentation in [Building System](Building-system.md) and [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md).

## Wolf and Creature AI

These findings come from the wolf-pack prototype, built against the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md), the [Tell](Glossary.md) entry in the glossary, and section 7 of [Taming and Control (Technical)](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md>), and play-tested in first person against packs of five and four wolves. Prototype module: `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Creatures/` and its `Wolf/` folder.

**The tell must come from the edge of view (18 October 2026).** The [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) makes the dropped tail and low shoulders the player's cue to turn and brace, and the natural reading — the wolf deepest in the player's blind spot attacks — puts that tell behind a first-person camera where nobody can see it, so the knockdown becomes unavoidable. The prototype chooses its lunger from the band between 50 and 110 degrees off the player's view direction, at the edge of vision, and only falls back to a wolf outside the band when none is in it. Facing the lunging wolf within 45 degrees braces: the bite still lands for half its force, but there is no knockdown. The proposal states in the wolf entry that the lunge comes from the corner of the eye, and names facing the wolf as the brace.

**Attacks come in waves of feints and lunges (18 October 2026).** A single lunge every few seconds made the pack feel passive, and a player who did nothing was left standing among idle wolves. The prototype sends a wave every 1,5 to 3,5 seconds once the circle has closed: the one or two wolves the player is looking at dash in, snap, and peel away, and while the player is fixed on them a third wolf gives the tell and lunges from the side. Once six or more wolves are fighting, a second lunger follows a second later from the opposite flank. One wolf always holds back, so a wave never empties the circle. This is the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md)'s "press from the sides while a player is fixed on the wolf in front" made concrete, and the proposal writes the wave into its *Pack Tactics*.

**Closer, faster, and never still (18 October 2026).** A pack that watched from 18 metres and closed its circle from 15 to 5,5 metres over twelve seconds read as hesitant, and a wolf that only trotted could not keep up with a running player. The prototype watches from 12 metres, closes from 10 to 4,5 metres in six seconds, trots at 4 metres a second, and sprints at 9,5 metres a second against a running player's 6 for about eight seconds, after which it can only trot until it has recovered; the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md)'s "outruns a running player over short distances before it tires" holds at those numbers. Stalking wolves take cover behind trunks and rocks near their place in the circle and pace about it, and a wolf the player walks straight at gives ground sideways, swinging round towards the player's back, so a pack the player charges splits and flows round instead of retreating in a line. The proposal adds the distances and the flowing retreat to the wolf entry.

**The leader is the easiest wolf to hit (18 October 2026).** The leader holds the front and fixes the player with its gaze, which also puts it in front of the player's spear. With the pack breaking when the leader fell below half its health, two spear thrusts ended every fight. The prototype breaks the pack only when the leader has lost three quarters of its health. The proposal gives "wounding the leader breaks the pack's nerve" that weight, a serious wound rather than a scratch, and asks whether the leader should also hang a step further back than the circle, as it does in the prototype at 1,3 times the circle's radius.

**A howl does not summon rival packs (18 October 2026).** "A howl from any wolf calls nearby wolves into the fight" first pulled a second, fully led pack into the hunt, which put two leaders and nine wolves round one player and blurred whose morale held. In the prototype only leaderless wolves answer — the remnant of a pack whose leader fell, or a lone wolf — and they fight as followers of the pack that howled. A pack with its own leader ignores another pack's howl and will not hunt prey that another pack is already hunting. The proposal says which wolves a howl calls in the wolf entry.

**A broken pack leaves (18 October 2026).** A pack whose nerve broke fled, then hovered at watching distance in plain sight, which looked like confusion rather than fear. The prototype has a broken pack, or one that has fled within the last minute, withdraw to about 30 metres from any player it sees, keeping to the trees, and the same holds when a pack meets a party larger than itself. The proposal adds that withdrawal to the wolf entry's paragraph on abandoning a fight.

**Fire slows the hunt without ending it (18 October 2026).** A torch that made wolves keep their distance entirely would make the player immune, while a light the circle ignored would mean nothing. The prototype gives a torch a 6-metre light and a campfire an 8-metre one, each with a further 2 metres in which wolves still feel it. Fear grows while a wolf stands in that zone and settles when it leaves; a frightened wolf backs out of the light and will not attack until it has calmed, and enough fire sends it running. In play the circle paces at the edge of the light and attacks far less often, which is the hesitation the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) describes. The proposal records the radii and the fear thresholds, hesitation at 0,35 and recovery at 0,25 on a scale from calm to panic, in section 7.3 of the taming technical guide.

**A StateTree brain could not be built with the available tooling (18 October 2026).** Section 7.1 of [Taming and Control (Technical)](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md>) hosts each creature's brain as a StateTree. The editor tooling the prototype uses can inspect StateTree assets but not author them, so the wolf's brain is a C++ state machine whose states map one to one onto StateTree states, with every decision in pure functions that unit tests drive. The proposal keeps StateTree as the target and adds to the guide that a species' decisions — which state wins, which slot and which attacker the pack picks — live in plain functions the tree's conditions and tasks call, so they stay testable without a running world.

**Perception on the pawn works (18 October 2026).** Section 7.1 leaves open whether the AI Perception component behaves on the creature pawn as it does on a controller. It does: the perception system casts sight from the owning pawn's eyes, registers the senses whatever the owner is, and only leaves the update binding to the owner. Wolves with perception on the pawn noticed players by sight in single player and on a dedicated server. The proposal closes the prototyping item in section 7.1.

**Bite checks must not read bones on a dedicated server (18 October 2026).** A dedicated server does not refresh the bone transforms of a mesh nobody renders, so a bite test placed at the head bone hits wherever the reference pose left the head. The prototype tests a sphere in front of the creature's body instead. The proposal adds the rule to the guide's creature section: server-side hit tests for creature attacks use the actor's own transform, never animated bones.

**Tells travel as replicated state, not as events (18 October 2026).** A tell is the one animation a player must never miss. Sent as an unreliable broadcast, a dropped packet loses it; the prototype replicates the current action with a sequence counter instead, and a client starts the animation when the value arrives. With a tenth of the packets dropped and 150 milliseconds of lag, the client saw the tell before every lunge. The proposal adds this to the replicated behaviour state in section 7.1.

**Every creature sees every other creature (18 October 2026).** Pawns register as sight targets by default, so each wolf's sight also tracks every other wolf in range, only to discard them, and the cost grows with the square of the creatures nearby. The proposal adds to the server budgets in section 8.2 that creatures and players carry a team affiliation and a creature's sight detects only what it treats as a threat; the cost itself has not been profiled.
