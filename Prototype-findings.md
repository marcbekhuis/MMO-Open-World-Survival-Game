# Prototype Findings

This is a working file, not part of the design proper. It collects what the companion Unreal Engine 5.8 prototype, `MmoSurvivalGame`, has learned by building the design: places where an idea did not survive play, needed a number the documents do not give, or turned out to work better another way. The prototype reads this document as canon and never edits the design files on its own; instead, every session in it records its findings here, and the findings move into the design documents and their `(Technical)` companions once the user accepts them. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for how this file fits the wider workflow, and [Design-backlog.md](Design-backlog.md) for questions the design itself still has to answer.

## How entries work

Each finding sits under the system it concerns, newest last. An entry names what the prototype tried, what play or testing showed, and the change it proposes, with links to the documents that change would touch. The date is the day the prototype established the finding, and the code path points at the prototype module where it can be seen. An entry stays here until its change has been written into the design or the user has rejected it; then it is removed, so this file holds only what is still waiting for a decision.

Modelling and content-creation sessions, including future ChatGPT and Codex sessions, may also add feedback here when the source design lacks information needed to produce the intended result, or when existing context is difficult to find. Name the missing or overlooked context, the assumption made, its observed consequence, and the document or link that would address it. Distinguish a missing design decision from an implementation mistake or a failure to read existing guidance. Such feedback remains a proposal until accepted; it does not authorise changes to the design documents themselves.

## Inventory

Findings from the prototype's inventory, world items and inventory screen, in `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Inventory/` with the screen in `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/UI/`.

**Every carried stack has an identity, and the hand and the hotbar point at it (10 November 2026).** The prototype gives each stack on a body a server-allocated number that lasts the session. Being in hand is not a place but a reference to one of those stacks, which stays where it is carried, so a ration readied from the belt pouch is still in the pouch while it is eaten. A hotbar slot holds the same reference, and a key press sends the item's number rather than the slot's, so a slot that is being reassigned while the press travels cannot make the client and the server ready different items. A container belongs to the item that provides it, which is why a dropped pack keeps what is in it. On a dedicated server at 150 milliseconds of lag, readying, eating to the last bite, dropping, merging, bundling, searching, taking and putting all ended with the client and the server holding the same stacks after every step. The proposal is an `Inventory (Technical).md` companion that sets out this model, with the stack identity, the ownership of containers, the hand as a reference and the predicted ready that reads only a snapshot taken when it begins, so that [Inventory](Inventory.md) and [Actions](Actions.md#hands-items-and-readiness) can stay at the level of play.

**A base capacity of 40 kilograms (10 November 2026).** [Inventory](Inventory.md#weight-and-strength-mechanics) gives the four burden tiers as shares of capacity but no starting capacity. The prototype uses 40 kilograms before any Carry Weight. The starting outfit of belt and pouch, sling strap, small pack, gambeson, spear, torch, bandages and rations weighs 9,9 kilograms, a quarter of that; a breastplate takes it to 18,9 (0,47) and a 25-kilogram log in the hands to 43,9 (1,10), over the limit, where a held sprint gives a 315 centimetres a second jog. Hauling a log therefore costs the sprint for a lightly armoured character and not for a bare one, which reads well. The proposal is to state 40 kilograms as the starting base in [Inventory](Inventory.md#weight-and-strength-mechanics) and in [Player Progression](Player.md) next to the Carry Weight step.

**The sling is a place of its own, and the belt is one pouch of four slots (10 November 2026).** [Inventory](Inventory.md#equipment) puts the sling over the pack on the back. The prototype wears the sling strap as its own equipment place, with one slot, so a character can carry a spear without a pack and take a pack off without losing the spear; the belt provides a single pouch of four slots that stands for everything at the hip. Both kept the readying times of [Inventory](Inventory.md#reach-and-the-hotbar) exact (0,415 seconds for a pouch bandage, 1,30 for the slung spear, 3,045 for a pack ration). The proposal is to name the sling as a separate place in the equipment list, and to say whether a belt carries one pouch or several.

**How loud worn armour is (10 November 2026).** [Armour](Armour.md#weight-breath-and-sound) says mail rings and plate clanks but gives no figure. The prototype multiplies the range at which each footstep is heard ([Movement (Technical)](Movement%20(Technical).md#93-noise), section 9.3) by one plus the sum of the worn pieces' additions: 0,30 for a mail hauberk and 0,15 each for a breastplate, an open helm and greaves, so a full kit is heard 1,75 times as far and a jog carries 26 metres instead of 15. Padded and leather pieces add nothing. These are untuned starting values; the proposal is to give them in [Armour](Armour.md#weight-breath-and-sound) and to let section 9.3 name the factor.

**Starting numbers for wear (10 November 2026).** [Inventory](Inventory.md#wear-and-repair) says a small share of each loss comes off the maximum. The prototype takes a tenth of every condition loss off the maximum as well. The spear loses 0,02 condition per joule it delivers, so a 75-joule thrust costs 1,5 of its 100 and a spear lasts about 65 thrusts before it breaks, and at zero condition its strikes stop; a wolf's 40-joule lunge took 0,35 condition off the gambeson it struck. A broken item cannot be readied or used. The proposal is to record a tenth as the starting share and these per-joule rates as the first values for weapons and armour.

**The times of picking up and searching (10 November 2026).** [Actions](Actions.md#interacting-with-the-world) describes quick and timed interactions without figures. The prototype picks an item up or puts a log down in 0,3 seconds, searches a bundle in 1,5 and a chest in 2,0, all within 2 metres of reach. Letting go before the search completes cancels it and opens nothing; once open, the window holds until it is closed or the searcher walks out of reach. On the server the searched bundle disappears when emptied, which ends the search. The proposal is to give these as starting values in [Actions](Actions.md#interacting-with-the-world).

**Rearranging the inventory is a round trip (10 November 2026).** [Inventory](Inventory.md#reach-and-the-hotbar) says items move the moment they are placed. In the prototype every move, drop, take and put is a request the server checks against its own stacks, and the client sees the result about one round trip later; only readying and using an item are predicted. Whether that delay can be felt on the inventory screen has not yet been tried over a network. The proposal is to say in the technical companion that rearranging is authoritative and unpredicted, and to keep "the moment they are placed" in the design as the intent.

**Moving a small item into the hands is readying it (11 November 2026).** [Inventory](Inventory.md#reach-and-the-hotbar) lets items move between hands, pouches, sling and pack the moment they are placed, and also gives the hands a ready time of zero. Taken together, a ration or a spear could be dragged from the pack into the hands instantly and then used at once, skipping the three seconds of fetching it from the pack that readying is meant to cost. The prototype therefore refuses a move into the hands for any item that has a container it belongs in; only a large item, or a worn item being taken off, can be moved there, and everything else reaches the hands by readying. The proposal is to say so in [Inventory](Inventory.md#reach-and-the-hotbar): the inventory view rearranges containers instantly, but the hands are filled only by readying.

**Nothing is taken through a wall (11 November 2026).** [Actions](Actions.md#interacting-with-the-world) speaks of reach but not of sight. The first version of the prototype measured only the distance and the facing, so an item or a chest behind a wall or on the floor above, within two metres, could be picked up or searched through it. The prototype now requires a clear line from the eyes to the middle of the target, on the client and on the server. The proposal is to state in [Actions](Actions.md#interacting-with-the-world) that an interaction needs both reach and a clear line of sight, which also matters for the claims and chests of [Building](Building-system.md).

**The player fills the hotbar, and the interface names the keys (11 November 2026).** The first prototype put every newly reachable item on the first free hotbar slot. In play this was annoying: the player wants to choose what sits on the hotbar and in what order. The same test found the screen's clickable text rows unclear next to Ark's or Conan Exiles' slot grids, and missed any word on which button does what: the pickup prompt said `Pick up Spear` without its key, and with a bandage in hand the mouse buttons gave no sign of what they would do or why nothing happened. The prototype's next phase rebuilt all three. Nothing reaches the hotbar by itself: the starting outfit puts the spear, a bandage and the torch on the first three slots, and from then on the player drags an item onto a slot, drags one slot onto another to swap them, or drags a slot off the screen to empty it; an item that leaves quick reach still leaves its slot. The inventory is a screen of square slots in three columns, the worn containers' grids on the left, the character with its equipment places around it in the middle, and burden, armour per region and vitals on the right, or the searched chest's grid while a search is open. A stack dragged onto a different item swaps the two when each fits the other's place, a stack dragged off the screen is dropped on the ground, a hover shows the item's description, mass, condition and uses, and a right click offers what applies to the item, readying a pack item among them. Every prompt now carries its key (`[F] Search Chest`), the line above the hotbar names what each button does with the item in hand (`[LMB] Thrust  [RMB] Guard`, leaving out the interact key while a target is focused, because the prompt already names it), and a refused action shows its reason there for 1,5 seconds (`No wound to bandage`, `Hands full`); a refusal the player did not cause, such as an empty hotbar slot, shows nothing. A loaded pack is never dropped, swapped or put into a chest from the screen. In the first play of the screen, dragging an item from the pack onto the hotbar did nothing and said nothing, so the rule that the hotbar holds only what is within quick reach read as a fault; the screen now marks such a slot red while the item is dragged over it and says why on release (`Only the hands, belt pouch, back sling and quiver go on the hotbar`), and it does the same for every move the rules refuse, such as a spear into the belt pouch; a refusal only the server can judge (a busy hand, a loaded pack put into a chest, a stack another player took first) now comes back with its reason too. The rest of the physical mouse pass, dragging and the right-click menu at different interface scales, has not yet been played. The proposal stands: state in [Inventory](Inventory.md#reach-and-the-hotbar) that the player chooses what sits on the hotbar and in what order, within quick reach, and in [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md#clean-core-depth-on-demand) that prompts and the held item's actions name their keys, with the refusal line and its 1,5 seconds as the starting value.

**The torch as a carried item (10 November 2026).** With the torch moved from a debug switch into the belt pouch, it lights when readied (0,52 seconds) and goes out when put away, and [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) packs still first hesitate 2,3 seconds after it lights, as the fear rate of 0,15 a second needs to reach 0,35. A knocked-down player cannot ready anything, so a player struck down at the moment of reaching for the torch does not light it until they are up again; one test run lost the whole torch stretch that way. The question for [Actions](Actions.md#hands-items-and-readiness) is whether a downed character may still draw a light or a knife from the belt, which would make the torch a way out of a mauling and not only a way to avoid one.

**How the character in the inventory screen is lit (11 November 2026).** [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md#clean-core-depth-on-demand) lists the inventory as a surface that opens on demand but says nothing about the character shown in it. The prototype shows the player's own body, live and in its idle pose, in the middle of the screen between the equipment places, by rendering a copy of it that stands fifty metres above the player in open sky, lit by the same sun and sky. In the daylight sandbox this reads well. It also means the figure is lit as if outdoors at noon even when the player stands in a cave, inside a house or at night, so the screen can contradict the darkness the art direction asks for. The question for [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md#clean-core-depth-on-demand) is whether the character view should show the body as it is lit where it stands, which keeps a night dark even in the menu, or in a neutral studio light that always shows the gear clearly.
