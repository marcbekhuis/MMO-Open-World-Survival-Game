# Prototype Findings

This is a working file, not part of the design proper. It collects what the companion Unreal Engine 5.8 prototype, `MmoSurvivalGame`, has learned by building the design: places where an idea did not survive play, needed a number the documents do not give, or turned out to work better another way. The prototype reads this document as canon and never edits the design files on its own; instead, every session in it records its findings here, and the findings move into the design documents and their `(Technical)` companions once the user accepts them. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for how this file fits the wider workflow, and [Design-backlog.md](Design-backlog.md) for questions the design itself still has to answer.

## How entries work

Each finding sits under the system it concerns, newest last. An entry names what the prototype tried, what play or testing showed, and the change it proposes, with links to the documents that change would touch. The date is the day the prototype established the finding, and the code path points at the prototype module where it can be seen. An entry stays here until its change has been written into the design or the user has rejected it; then it is removed, so this file holds only what is still waiting for a decision.

## Creatures

Findings from the prototype's shared creature module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Creatures/`, which the wolf now builds on.

**Creature actions are gameplay tags, and the species data holds the body, senses and attacks (1 November 2026).** The prototype moved the wolf's body onto a shared creature base and gave it a species data asset, named after the `UCreatureSpeciesData` of [Taming and Control (Technical)](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#21-species-data>). Section 7.1 of that guide sketches the replicated action as `FCreatureActionState { uint8 Action; uint8 Sequence; }` over an `ECreatureAction` enum. The prototype replicates a gameplay tag under `Creature.Action` (`Creature.Action.Tell`, `.Lunge`, `.Bite`, `.Howl`, `.Hit.Front` and so on) with the same sequence counter, and each species maps the tags it uses to montages in its data. A new species then adds actions in data instead of growing one enum shared by every creature. On a dedicated server at 150 milliseconds the client received every tell, lunge, bite and howl of the wolf encounter by tag. The species asset also proved the natural home for what sections 7.3 and 7.6 already call species data: sight radius and half-angle, hearing range and sense memory, threat memory, fear settle rate and fear sensitivity, follower and leader health and the leader's size, and one attack shape per striking action (a sphere's radius and its reach in front of the body). The attack shapes are a list of rows that each name their action, not a map keyed by the action, because the server's by-name tuning overrides cannot address map entries in Unreal Engine 5.8. The prototype then moved the brain's shared parts onto the same asset: the hesitate, recover and flee thresholds of [section 7.3](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#73-perception-and-fear>), how far a hesitating creature backs out past a fire's edge, and how the brain asks for paths. Proposed change: in [section 7.1](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#71-server-only-brains>) make the action field an `FGameplayTag`, and in [section 2.1](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#21-species-data>) list the body, leader, perception, awareness, fear, brain movement and attack-shape groups beside the taming data.

**Team filtering made wolves notice a player faster (1 November 2026).** The prototype gave every body a team and set each creature's senses to detect only teams it is hostile to, as [section 8.2](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#82-server-budgets>) of the taming guide asks. Teams are gameplay tags with a one-way table of hostile pairs in configuration, so the wolf is hostile to players without players needing an attitude towards wolves, and a body with no team is neutral to everyone. On a dedicated server with fourteen wolves in the sandbox, a pack spawned 20 metres from a standing player noticed it 0,2 to 0,3 seconds after spawning in seven runs, against 1,3 to 3,0 seconds before, when every wolf's sight also tracked every other wolf. The guide's note that the saving has not been profiled can take this as a first, indirect measure: the time-sliced sight checks reach the prey sooner when they no longer spend themselves on packmates. One caveat for the guide: the engine caches a creature's team when its senses register, so the taming bond that changes a creature's side at runtime must refresh both its senses and its registration as a seen target.

**A plain deer as test prey, and what a wolf hunt of a herd showed (1 November 2026).** The design has no ordinary deer; [Ancient Deer](Creatures/Ancient-deer.md) is a magical tundra species. The prototype added a plain deer herd as test prey (module `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Creatures/Deer/`): six deer that graze behind a leader, stand alert facing a threat within 25 metres and flee as one within 15 metres or when any deer is hurt, with a sprint faster than a wolf's (11 against 9,5 metres a second) that lasts 5 seconds instead of 8. Wolves treat deer as prey like players. In two dedicated-server hunts the pack ran the herd until its deer were winded, switched to a winded deer as the [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) target rules intend, knocked down two or three and killed one or two. A first rule that let any deer strike a wolf within 2,5 metres made the herd stand and fight: 42 antler strikes in 40 seconds, a dead wolf and a broken pack, so the prototype now lets only a winded deer, which can no longer outrun the pack, turn and strike; even so, winded deer killed a wolf in one hunt and broke the pack's nerve in the other. Proposed change: decide whether the world needs a plain deer (or another common grazer) as the ordinary prey of [Wolf](Creatures/Wolf.md) packs and players, and if so add it under Creatures with the herd, flight and cornered-strike behaviour above; the [Ancient Deer](Creatures/Ancient-deer.md) entry can take the same rule that a deer fights only when it can no longer flee.

**A brain's path requests need a back-off after failures (1 November 2026).** On a dedicated server the navigation mesh is built after the server starts, which took between 20 and 112 seconds in the prototype's sandbox, and every path request made before it is ready fails at once. The wolf brain asked again on its next tick, so the nine wolves of the two resting packs made about 1500 to 1850 failed requests in a two-minute run, most of all the requests the server handled. Waiting a quarter of a second after a failure and doubling the wait per failure in a row, up to 2 seconds, cut that to 50 to 90 failed requests and roughly halved all requests, without changing how the pack hunted. A distance-scaled re-goal with a cooldown between requests, the obvious throttle, saved nothing on top, even against a jogging target. Proposed change: [section 7.5](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#75-navigation-by-body-size>) and the scale budget of [section 8.2](<Taming-and-control (Technical).md#82-server-budgets>) gain the rule that a creature backs off its path requests after failures, and that a region server's creatures expect failed requests until its navigation data is built or loaded.

## Populations and Spawning

Findings from the prototype's population module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Population/`, which brings creature groups into the sandbox through spawn zones and puts them to sleep away from players.

**Sleeping groups must be woken only by players and by groups a player woke (2 November 2026).** [Populations and Spawning](Populations-and-spawning.md#every-creature-is-someone) lets an awake creature wake a group it could sense, so that a pack drawn out by a hunter can still find the herd in the next valley. Taken literally, that rule keeps a herd and a pack awake for good once a player has woken either: each is an awake creature that can sense the other. In a dense region the waking also spreads from group to group. The prototype therefore wakes groups in two passes: first every group within its wake distance of a player, then every group within its wake distance of a hostile group that a player woke, and no further. A sleeping creature also stops counting as a threat that keeps another group awake; before that change a herd placed within sight of a sleeping pack never slept again. On a dedicated server at 150 milliseconds, a player jogging at 3,5 metres a second woke two herds at 89,8 and 87,8 metres (their senses plus a 30 metre margin, checked once a second), and their pack one step later through the herds. Both herds slept again while the player stood 300 metres away, and the whole region was asleep once the player had left. Proposed change: in [Every Creature Is Someone](Populations-and-spawning.md#every-creature-is-someone), say that only a group a player has woken can wake another, and only one step deep. In the perceiver index of [Server Architecture (Technical)](<Server-architecture (Technical).md#62-visibility-overlap-detection>), count only awake creatures as perceivers.

**A zone's memory of its kill sites must outlast its quiet period (2 November 2026).** The prototype first kept a zone's recent deaths for exactly its quiet period. A zone becomes due for new arrivals once it has been below its minimum for that same period, so the deaths that emptied it were forgotten at the moment it became due: in the first gate run a new herd appeared 7 metres from where the last one had been killed. With a separate memory of five minutes against a one-minute quiet period, the next run placed both new herds 22 metres or more from every death. The design text now says the memory outlasts the quiet period. Proposed change: give the kill-site memory its own server setting beside the quiet period in [Load and Server Settings](Populations-and-spawning.md#load-and-server-settings), and say there that it must be the longer of the two.

## Movement

Findings from the prototype's movement module, `Source/MmoSurvivalGame/Movement/`, which every walking body builds on.

**A vault needs a base time, and traversal clips must be cut to fit (3 November 2026).** [Vaulting and Mantling](Movement.md#vaulting-and-mantling) gives a vault half a second for every metre of height, 0,30 seconds for a 60-centimetre wall and 0,55 seconds for one at waist height. In play that vault felt too fast: the body was over a knee-high wall before the crossing read as a vault, and the motion-captured vault clip of 1,60 seconds, about a third of it a run-in before the hand reaches the top, played at 2,9 to 5,3 times its authored speed. The prototype now gives a vault a base time of 0,35 seconds plus the half second a metre, 0,65 seconds at 60 centimetres and 0,9 at waist height, and plays the clip only from the hand reaching the wall to the landing, which runs at 1,1 to 1,5 times its speed. The vault still leaves the wall with four fifths of its speed, so a sprint keeps its pace; the crossing itself is slower. The mantle clips last 1,97 and 2,90 seconds and climb exactly 1 and 2 metres, so a 1,50-metre ledge plays the 2-metre climb at up to 2,5 times its speed, a climb half a metre higher than the edge, and no clip fits a climb-over, which borrows the waist-high vault. Proposed change: give the vault a base time in [Vaulting and Mantling](Movement.md#vaulting-and-mantling), as the mantle already has, and have [section 10 of Movement (Technical)](<Movement (Technical).md#10-animation-and-first-person-presentation>) require traversal animation authored or cut to the traversal times: a vault from hand plant to landing, mantles in height steps of about half a metre, and hands placed on the edge by IK or motion warping rather than by the clip.

**Onlookers need the traversal clip and its duration, not only the movement mode (3 November 2026).** A traversal reaches other clients only as a movement mode in the prototype. That was enough for one clip played at its own pace, but not for a clip chosen by kind and height and timed to a duration that onlookers cannot work out without the measured obstacle. The prototype adds two fields to the view struct that [section 8](<Movement (Technical).md#8-creatures-server-driven-bodies>) already replicates to everyone but the owner: the clip's entry in the body's list of traversal clips (one byte) and the traversal's duration in milliseconds (two bytes). A mantle's clip also plays at two rates, so that its climb ends when the capsule reaches the top of its rise. In networked runs at 150 milliseconds of latency, an onlooking client played the same clip, duration and rates as the owner and the server for both traversals it was close enough to see. Proposed change: list the two fields with the view struct in section 8, and describe the clip choice and the two-rate timing in section 10.
