# Deep Caverns

The Deep Caverns are the world beneath the world: a vast network of natural caves, chasms and flooded passages that runs under the mountains and much of the frontier, reached through cave mouths, sinkholes and mine shafts that break into it from above. There is no daylight here, no weather and no horizon, only the light a traveller carries and the faint glow of the cavern itself. The biome strikes a note of claustrophobic wonder, by turns beautiful and suffocating, and the travel problem it poses is simple to state and hard to solve: every light that lets a player see also lets everything else see the player.

## Terrain and Atmosphere

The caverns are built in layers. Near the surface, narrow passages and low crawlways open into galleries the size of a cathedral, their ceilings lost in the dark, their floors broken by stalagmites, rubble slopes and sudden drops. Deeper down the passages widen into chasms crossed only by natural stone bridges and ledges, and the air grows still, damp and cold. The whole system is a maze, with dead ends, loops and side passages that double back on themselves, and it rewards anyone who charts it through the [Cartography](../Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md) skill, because a map drawn underground is worth far more than one drawn in the sun.

Light comes from the cavern itself only in places. Bioluminescent fungus grows in pale blue and green patches across walls and ceilings, brightest where water runs, and seams of exposed leyline crystal glow with a cold, steady violet where the world's mana runs close beneath the rock (see [Magic](../Magic.md)). Between those islands of light the dark is total. A traveller can cross a glowing gallery and see its whole shape, then step into a passage where a torch shows only the next few metres. Carrying a light is a necessity and a giveaway at once, and the choice of when to cover it is one of the biome's constant decisions.

Underground rivers run through the lower reaches, black, fast and close to freezing, carving their own passages and filling some galleries into still, dark lakes. They are roads as well as obstacles, since a raft or a strong swimmer can cover in minutes what would take an hour of climbing, but they run into flooded tunnels and sumps where the ceiling meets the water.

## Resources

The caverns are rich for those who can work them. Leyline crystal, the most valuable thing the deep holds, feeds enchantment and the mana work of [Magic](../Magic.md). Metal ores and gemstones lie closer to hand than anywhere on the surface, which is why mines are dug toward the deep and sometimes break into it. Cave fungus offers food, lamp oil and alchemical reagents for [Crafting](../Crafting.md), and the rivers carry pale, blind fish that are a staple for anyone camping underground. Beyond what grows and lies in the rock, the caverns hold what has been lost in them: the gear of the dead, and the hoards of the goblins who collect it.

## Creatures

The best-known inhabitants are the [Golden Goblins](../Creatures/Golden-goblin.md), pale scavengers whose colonies can number a thousand around a hoard of gathered shine. They avoid a fair fight and flee light, but they strip the dead with alarming speed and swarm anyone who threatens their treasure, so their presence turns every death in the caverns into a race. The damp hollows where carrion collects are also the haunt of [Neutral Slimes](../Creatures/Neutral-slime.md) of the [Slime](../Creatures/Slime.md) family, which compete with the goblins for the same bodies and sit motionless beside them in the dark. In the deepest maze-like passages a [Minotaur](../Creatures/Minotaur.md) can claim a whole knot of tunnels as its territory, and in those narrow, twisting spaces its charge is the most frightening sound the caverns make.

## Hazards and Travel

The dark is the first hazard and the one beneath all the others. Torches and lamps burn out, and a traveller who runs out of light in an unlit stretch of tunnel is blind among things that are not. The cold and damp of the lower reaches feed the exposure that [Survival](../Survival.md) tracks, and the rivers can sweep a careless swimmer into a sump with no air above it. Rockfalls and collapses can seal a passage behind a group, loose scree hides drops into chasms, and in a maze where every gallery looks alike, simply getting lost is a serious danger. Sound carries far underground, so fighting, digging and shouting all travel further than a player expects, and anything listening knows where they are.

Players adapt by travelling prepared: enough light and fuel for the trip back as well as the trip in, rope for the drops, a map or a guide, and a plan for what happens if someone dies, because a body left in golden goblin country will not be there for long.

## Settlement Patterns

No one settles the caverns in the ordinary sense. The [Dwarven Settlements](../Structures/Dwarven-settlements.md) carved into the mountains push their deepest workings toward them for ore and crystal, and the breaches those mines make are the main way the caverns and the surface meet, along with the cave mouths and sinkholes of the [Mountain Range](Mountain-range.md). Player groups build fortified camps at cavern entrances and in defensible galleries near crystal seams, outposts that depend on supply from above and exist to hold a claim on what the deep gives up.
