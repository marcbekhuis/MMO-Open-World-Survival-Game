# Coast

The Coast is the world's shoreline, the broken margin where land gives way to salt water: pale beaches and shingle coves, reef flats, tidal caves, sea stacks standing off the headlands, and in the shallows beyond them the wreck fields where ships have piled up for generations. It is a biome of thresholds. Travellers come here to fish, to salvage, and to board ships, and they find a place that changes shape twice a day and hides its dangers behind fog, pretty music, and water that looks shallower than it is. Everything the open sea sends toward land ends up here, which makes the Coast both the gateway to the [Deep Ocean](Deep-ocean.md) and the place where the ocean's wreckage and warnings wash ashore.

## Terrain and Atmosphere

The tide is the coast's clock, and the land it governs is never the same shape for long. At low water the reef flats lie open and walkable, studded with tide pools, and the mouths of tidal caves stand dry enough to enter; a few hours later the same ground is under a metre of moving water and the caves have sealed themselves. Sea stacks, columns of dark rock twenty to sixty metres high, rise off the headlands like the ruins of a drowned wall, ringed with surf and crowded with nesting birds. Between the headlands lie the coves, sheltered crescents of sand where the water stays calm and a boat can be beached, and they are the coast's natural harbours and its favourite ambush grounds. Offshore, in the shallows where reefs and currents gather drifting timber, the wreck fields run for hundreds of metres, a graveyard of hulls, masts, and broken cargo that glints in clear water and looms out of the fog in bad weather. Gulls, surf, and creaking rigging fill the soundscape, and fog banks come in off the water and swallow the horizon while the shore is still in sun.

## Resources

The Coast is the world's larder and its scrapyard. Fishing is at its most productive in the reef shallows and along the tide line, shellfish and crabs can be gathered by hand at low water, and the reef oysters hold pearls that the markets of [Economy and Trade](../Economy.md) price far above their weight. Salt is raked from evaporation pans above the tide line and cut from the crust of the sea stacks, a staple that every settlement in the world needs for preserving food and curing hides. The wreck fields supply the rest: timber, rope, iron fittings, and the cargo of ships that never reached harbour, recovered through the salvage discipline of [Skills](../Skills.md) and turned into hull repairs, rigging, and building stock through [Crafting](../Crafting.md). Salvage is the coast's gamble, because the richest wrecks lie deepest in the fields, farthest from help and closest to whatever lives among them.

## Creatures

The [Mermaid](../Creatures/Mermaid.md) is the coast's signature predator, working the reefs, cove mouths, and sea stacks and the wreck fields beyond them.

[Seabirds](../Creatures/Birds.md) fill the sky above the shore: gulls and terns working the tide line, cormorants drying their wings on the rocks, and the long-winged birds that follow ships out over the water. They are the coast's early-warning system, read the way they are read anywhere else. A cloud of gulls circling one patch of water marks a carcass or a fresh wreck, and a crowd of them fleeing inland ahead of a dark horizon tells a sailor that a storm is coming before the glass does.

Shore life supplies the ambience and the hunting: crabs and shellfish in the pools, seals and sea lions hauled out on the rocks, and shoals of fish running the reef channels. Now and then a [Titan Turtle](../Creatures/Titan-turtle.md) walks down from the inland country and wades into the shallows, a slow hill of shell moving out over the reef flats. Beyond the last reef, the sea belongs to the [Kraken](../Creatures/Kraken.md).

## Hazards and Travel

The tide is the first hazard and the one a traveller can learn. Reef flats, tidal caves, and low causeways to the sea stacks are safe only while the water is out, and a player who lingers over a find past the turn is caught between rising water and a cliff. Charting the tide times, channels, and safe landings is exactly the knowledge that [Cartography](../Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md) turns into something other players will pay for. Riptides are the second hazard, running through the gaps in the reefs and pulling swimmers and small boats out past the breakers, where the water is deeper and the help further away. Fog is the third hazard, muffling sound, hiding rock and shoal from a helmsman, and covering the approach of anything that hunts from the water; it is also the weather in which a Mermaid's song carries furthest. Storms pile breakers onto the headlands and drive ships onto the reefs, which is how the wreck fields grow, and the [weather of the world](../Weather-time-and-seasons.md) decides in any given week how much of the shore is workable. A traveller here watches the water level, the fog, and the birds, and keeps a partner close, because a voice across the water is best answered by the person standing next to you.

## Settlement Patterns

The Coast supports three recurring kinds of settlement, each shaped by what the sea gives it. Fishing villages sit just above the tide line behind the dunes, their boats drawn up on the sand and their smokehouses and drying racks turning the daily catch into trade goods. Harbours take hold where a sheltered cove or a natural breakwater lets ships lie safely at anchor, growing into the trading hubs where cargo changes hands and crews are hired, and where the sea routes of [Travel and Mounts](../Travel-and-mounts.md) begin and end. Salvage camps are the rough cousins of both, temporary clusters of shelters and winches pitched on the shore nearest a wreck field, occupied for as long as the wrecks keep paying out. Their people know the local tides, and they keep taboos around night fishing and lone watches that the [Mermaid](../Creatures/Mermaid.md) gave them good reason to learn. The wider pattern of the world's settlements is set out in [Structures](../Structures.md).
