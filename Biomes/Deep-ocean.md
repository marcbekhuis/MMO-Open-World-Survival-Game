# Deep Ocean

Beyond the last reef the world becomes water. The Deep Ocean is the open sea in all its scale: rolling swells with no landmark to steer by, storm belts that build and break across days of sailing, and trenches where the seabed drops away into black water far below any keel. It is the most exposed biome in the game and the least forgiving. There is no ground to retreat to, no cover to hide behind, and no help within reach, so every crossing is a commitment made with the ship and the crew that are already aboard. It is also where the world's largest and rarest prizes lie, and the one home of the [Kraken](../Creatures/Kraken.md).

## Terrain and Atmosphere

The ocean has no terrain in the usual sense; it has weather, depth, and distance. Long swells roll under a sky that fills most of the view, the horizon is a clean line in every direction, and a ship becomes the only fixed point in a moving world. The colour of the water tells its depth: pale green over the shelf that leaves the [Coast](Coast.md), deepening to an ink blue that reads as bottomless where a trench opens below. Storm belts are the ocean's regions of trouble, broad bands of churning sky where wind and waves build for hours, and they are where the water feels most alive. Between them lie calms, when the swell flattens, the wind dies, and a sailing ship sits becalmed under a sky too bright to look at. At night the sea is dark, and in a storm faint lines of light sometimes move under the surface.

## Resources

The deep ocean pays in kind for the risk of reaching it. Its waters hold catches the shore never sees, large, heavy-bodied fish and the deep shoals that follow the trench edges, which reward the fishing discipline of [Skills](../Skills.md) and feed the tables and markets of [Economy and Trade](../Economy.md) with a quality that coastal waters cannot match. Far below, the trench walls and floors hold materials formed under crushing pressure: mineral crust, pressure-hardened shell, and mana-rich deposits that feed the highest tiers of [Crafting](../Crafting.md). They lie too deep and too dark to reach by diving, and they surface only with the [Kraken](../Creatures/Kraken.md) itself, which makes the greatest hunt of the sea also its richest harvest. Drifting wreckage from ships lost in the storm belts is the ocean's smaller, more common gift.

## Creatures

The [Kraken](../Creatures/Kraken.md) is the ocean's one great predator and its defining danger. It lives in the trenches, rises under ships caught in a storm above its territory, and appears rarely enough that most crossings never meet it. Its presence shapes how crews behave, and it is why a captain reads the sky and the trench charts with equal care. Elsewhere the open sea is full of ambient life that gives it depth without adding combat: whales and their calves surfacing alongside a hull, rays gliding beneath the swell, and dense shoals flashing silver in the water, with seabirds thinning out to a few long-winged wanderers a long way from shore. A crew that stays alert learns to read a change in this life: shoals sounding all at once or the whales suddenly gone from the water are the quiet signs that something larger is moving below.

## Hazards and Travel

Storms are the defining hazard. A storm belt raises breakers that can roll a poorly handled ship, snap masts, and strip the deck of anyone not lashed down, and it is the weather that draws the Kraken. The [weather system](../Weather-time-and-seasons.md) makes a storm something a crew sees building and can route around, at the cost of a longer, slower passage. There is no shelter here. A damaged ship has nowhere to put in and no one to repair it but its own crew, and a ship that goes down leaves its crew in open water, with the [Kraken](../Creatures/Kraken.md) file covering what becomes of the cargo, the survivors, and the wreck. Distance and navigation are hazards of their own: with no landmarks, a crew that mischarts its course or drifts in a calm can be days from where it believed itself to be, and the skill of [Cartography](../Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md) is what turns a route across the blank water into something a captain can trust. The ocean rewards the crew that arrives provisioned, armed, and holding a chart of where the trenches and storm belts lie.

## Settlement Patterns

Nothing is settled on the deep ocean in the ordinary sense, because nothing can stand on it. The only permanent homes are ships and floating outposts. A ship is a settlement in motion, carrying its crew, cargo, and stores across the water as the base of a guild or a trading house. A floating outpost is a player-built platform moored to a fixed point at sea under the rules of the [Building System](../Building-system.md), kept by a guild as a waystation, a refuge for damaged ships, and a forward base for a hunt, and it is the only fixed structure the ocean holds. Both are fragile and expensive, and both exist because of what the sea offers: trade routes, deep-water catches, and the chance at the Kraken. The settlements that feed them lie on the [Coast](Coast.md).
