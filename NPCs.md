# Non-Player Characters

Non-player characters are the human, elven, and dwarven people who make settlements feel inhabited rather than merely serviced. They are merchants, guards, craftspeople, trainers, travellers, farmers, performers, mages, and civilians, all tied into the routines, trade, danger, and reputation systems that make the world feel lived in.

## Roles and Routines

NPCs are readable by role at a glance while still feeling like people who belong to a place. Merchants move goods between cities and frontier settlements, guards hold gates and patrol roads, city mages maintain protective work around crowded hubs, and craftspeople visibly perform the labour that keeps a settlement functioning. Civilians such as farmers, bartenders, musicians, weavers, butchers, and builders add ordinary life around the services players use. Children appear only in safe, non-combat routines where they add life to urban spaces without becoming targets or rewards.

These roles scale with settlement size. A roadside outpost has a handful of armed guards, a trader, and a tired cook; a great city supports patrols, guild representatives, trainers, specialist vendors, craftsmen, performers, and city mages. The larger the settlement, the more its NPCs communicate its power, wealth, and vulnerabilities through what they are seen doing.

## Interaction and Services

NPCs provide the stable layer of play that lets the world support many lives within it. They offer quests, escort requests, protection work, delivery jobs, vendors, trainers, crafting stations, repair, rumours, and settlement services. The [Quest System](Quests.md) uses them as the face of local needs, while [Economy and Trade](Economy.md) uses merchants and craftsmen to anchor prices, stock basic goods, and give non-crafters a complete way to stay supplied.

Some NPC routines connect directly to wider systems. Travelling merchants create convoy routes that can be escorted, ambushed, taxed, or avoided. Guards and mages define the practical edge of a safe city, shaping how players behave around settlements and how the [Reputation System](Reputation-system.md) responds to theft, murder, defence, and public service. City mages use [Nature Growth](Magic/Spells/Earth/Nature-growth.md) around crowded starting areas to restore over-harvested local resources, with cooldowns and limits so the spell supports new-player density without becoming an exploitable production loop.

## Hired Guards

A base cannot be watched by its owners every hour, and a small group with a modest holding cannot post a player on every wall. Players can hire NPC guards from a settlement's guard captains and from the mercenary companies that work the roads, and set them to hold a gate, a wall-walk, or a storehouse. Hired guards are the ordinary answer to an empty base: they are there when the owners are asleep or away, they raise the alarm that [raids](Raids.md) otherwise take for granted, and alongside the bonded creatures of [Taming and Control](Taming-and-control.md) they are how a small group defends ground it could never hold with its own hands.

Their wages are upkeep. A guard is paid in coin and provisions on a regular round, the bill grows with their number and the quality of their arms and training, and it falls due whether or not the base is ever attacked. Loyalty rests on two things, pay and reputation. A guard who is paid on time, fed, and given a post worth holding stands their ground, while one whose wages fall behind first grumbles, then leaves the post, and in the worst case walks away carrying the gear they were issued. Reputation sets the terms before any of that: a captain will not sell guards to a notorious employer, a mercenary company asks more of a name it has heard bad things of, and a guard who learns that their employer has taken to raiding settlements or to slaving may quit on the spot. Hired guards are people with their own sense of when a fight is lost; they hold a position well and chase badly, and when the odds are hopeless they run, so a handful of them slows a raid but does not stop one.

## Enslaved Labour and Guards

The frontier's darker trades are open to players as they are to the NPCs who practise them. A player may enslave NPCs, taking them from the slaver caravans and bandit camps that deal in captives or buying them from those caravans, and set them to labour in a base or to stand guard on its walls. Children are never taken. Only NPCs can be enslaved: [Content and Tone](Content-and-tone.md#a-grim-world-not-a-cruelty-sandbox) keeps capture, enslavement, torture, and imprisonment off the table between players, and nothing here changes that.

Slavery is the cheapest labour in the world and the most expensive in standing. Its cost is paid in the [Reputation System](Reputation-system.md#enslaving-npcs), heavily and for as long as the captives are held, so a group that keeps a gang of slaves has chosen to be unwelcome in most places worth trading in.

What holds an enslaved NPC is coercion, and it behaves as the force that holds a creature in [slave bonding](Taming-and-control.md#slave-bonding) does. The captive works or fights only while pressure is kept on them, the threat of the lash, the chain, and an overseer's eye, and the pressure wears off the moment it is not renewed. It wears off while the owner is away as much as while they are present, so a base full of slaves needs hired guards or a standing watch to keep the pressure up. When it slips, the captive's nature decides what follows. A frightened labourer slips away in the night and takes the story of where they were held to the nearest settlement, a proud fighter turns on the overseers, and a slave who has been handed a weapon and a post on the wall is the most dangerous of all, because the arms that were meant to guard the base are now in the hands of someone with every reason to open the gate. A raid that breaks into a base also breaks the pressure there, and the slaves held inside may fight for the raiders, flee, or be freed. Unlike a bonded animal, an enslaved NPC is a person who remembers: freed or escaped captives carry their account into the world, and what they tell the settlements adds to the holder's name.

## World Events

Because NPCs move through the same world as players, they generate trouble as well as services. A merchant convoy delayed on the road, a guard patrol that fails to return, a village musician carrying rumours between inns, or a mage sent to repair depleted groves can all become the beginning of an event rather than a static menu. These events tie settlements to the wilderness around them: players meet civilisation on the road, not only behind walls.

NPCs share the player races' visual language so they feel like part of the same societies described in [Races](Races.md), with variety coming from clothing, armour, accessories, posture, animation, and the work they are performing. This makes each settlement legible as a place where people live, need things, fear things, and remember what players do.
