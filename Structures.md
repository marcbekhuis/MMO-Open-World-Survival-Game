# Structures

This document summarises the built environment of the world: great cities, the towns and farming hamlets that grow up around them, the independent settlements that answer to no realm, dungeons, ruins, and player-facing service locations. More detailed design notes for each settlement type live in the `Structures/` folder.

## Settlement Types

- **Great cities** - Capital-scale hubs for trade, travel, onboarding, and character spawns, several for each people; some are the seat of a realm. See [Great cities](Structures/Great-cities.md).
- **Human settlements** - Towns and villages scaled by population, local services, and surrounding trade routes. See [Human settlements](Structures/Human-settlements.md).
- **Elven settlements** - Tree-integrated communities focused on harmony with nature, forest magic, and hidden movement. See [Elven settlements](Structures/Elven-settlements.md).
- **Dwarven settlements** - Stone-hewn cities and forge-centres with strong crafting economies and underground access. See [Dwarven settlements](Structures/Dwarven-settlements.md).
- **Dungeons and ruins** - Ancient superstructures, buried complexes, and dangerous landmarks that hide treasures and challenges. See [Dungeons](Structures/Dungeons.md).

## How Settlements Spread

Settlements follow the logic of the land that feeds them. A great city eats far more than its walls could ever grow, so it sits at the centre of a broad belt of farmland, worked by people who walk out to their fields at dawn and home again at dusk. Farther out, that walk becomes too long to make every day. Farmers whose land lies beyond an easy return settle beside their fields instead, and so the edge of the farm belt is dotted with farming hamlets: a few houses gathered together, perhaps a chapel or a smithy, sending grain, livestock, and wool inward along the roads. Where roads cross and a market can pay its way, a hamlet grows into a village and a village into a fortified town, each one a little farther from the city and a little closer to the wild.

The pattern thins with distance. Heartland roads run past one farm after another; toward the edge of a realm the hamlets grow far apart, watchtowers take their place, and the last fields give way to the frontier, where almost no NPC has managed to settle at all. A player can read how far they are from safety in the countryside itself before any danger shows, and the pressure of [Survival](Survival.md), which rises with distance from safety, follows the same gradient. The farm belt also gives a city's hinterland its own stakes: hamlets that feed a great city are what bandits raid, what monsters stray into, and what a guild must protect if it wants the city's goodwill.

## Independent Settlements

Not every settlement answers to a realm. Outlaw towns grow where the law cannot reach, sheltering deserters, smugglers, debtors, and anyone a realm wants hanged, and they trade with whoever pays. Bandit camps sit within striking distance of the roads and the outlying hamlets and live off what they can take. Alongside them, trapper camps, hermit holds, and mining outposts cling on in the frontier through stubbornness alone. Together these make up most of the NPC settlement a player meets beyond a realm's borders, and they are sparse enough that the frontier's open ground belongs to whoever can build on it and hold it, which is where the player settlements of the [Building System](Building-system.md) take root. An independent settlement offers services a realm would not, at prices and risks a realm would not ask, and the [Reputation System](Reputation-system.md) decides how it treats each player who walks in.

## Design Role

Structures make the world easier to read. A distant tower, bridge, gate, mine entrance, or treetop hall tells players what kind of place they are approaching before the interface needs to explain it. Settlements provide services such as vendors, crafting stations, [quest givers](Quests.md), storage, social hubs, and [faction representatives](Guilds-and-factions.md), while dungeons and ruins create risk-heavy destinations for exploration and group play.

Settlement scale changes both function and atmosphere. A farming hamlet offers little beyond water, a dry barn, and news of whatever has been taking the sheep. A village adds basic food, repairs, and local rumours. A fortified town supports crafting specialists, guards, guild contacts, and trade. A great city is politically and economically important, with portals, high-value merchants, public gathering spaces, and race-specific onboarding, as set out in [Great cities](Structures/Great-cities.md).
