# Great Cities

Great cities are the largest settlements in the world: political, economic, and social centres where players begin, train, trade, and organise, each built on the scale of a capital. Some are the seat of a realm's ruler, but most are not; what makes a city great is its size and its services, not a crown. Every playable people has several, spread across the many realms of that people, so new characters begin scattered across the world rather than crowded behind a handful of gates, and the frontier is approached from many directions at once.

## Key Characteristics

Great cities host race-specific starting areas, tutorials, trainers, and early [quest hubs](../Quests.md). They concentrate high-tier merchants, specialty crafting stations, banking, storage, [auction systems](../Economy.md), and [faction representatives](../Guilds-and-factions.md). Each is a safe city, held by NPC guards strong enough to suppress player aggression within its walls, as set out in [Conflict and PvP](../Conflict-and-pvp.md). Portals link the great cities to one another, at a cost that keeps long-distance movement and local trade valuable. Guild halls, markets, taverns, public event areas, and announcement boards make them natural meeting points.

No great city stands alone. Each sits at the heart of its own farm belt and the ring of farming hamlets beyond it, the pattern described in [Structures](../Structures.md#how-settlements-spread), so the road out of a great city passes through working countryside long before it reaches the wild.

## Architecture by People

Each people builds its great cities in its own way, and a great city carries its people's identity whichever realm it belongs to. A human great city is a broad market-city built around roads, civic plazas, guild halls, and a central portal square: practical, crowded, and tied to trade. An elven great city is a vertical forest-city woven through enormous living trees, canopy bridges, archives, and nature-magic sanctuaries, rewarding vertical navigation and quiet discovery. A dwarven great city is a mountain or underground forge-city built around deep gates, grand halls, molten workshops, and mineral markets, made for permanence, craft, and defence.

## Starting Hubs

Every player begins in one of the great cities of the race they chose, and its first districts teach how that people moves, trades, and survives before the wilderness gets the chance to.

A human great city teaches flexibility and trade. Its first districts hold a central bazaar, a militia yard, basic crafting workshops, and road gates that open toward the farm belt and the lower-risk country beyond, and its early quests turn on caravans, roads, raided hamlets, and the ordinary business of a settlement holding its ground.

An elven great city teaches vertical navigation, careful movement, ranged combat, nature magic, and the reading of environmental cues. Its early quests lead through canopy paths, root-level gardens, and protected groves before players enter the deeper forest.

A dwarven great city teaches endurance, mining, crafting, heavy armour, and defensive play. New players begin among forge halls, training pits, and ore lifts, beside tunnel gates that lead out to exposed mountain passes and cave networks, and the first lesson the hold drives home is preparation before venturing out.

## Portal Cost

Portals between great cities exist to reunite friends across distant starts and to keep the early game from holding players apart for too long, never to serve as risk-free logistics. Their toll, cooldowns, and limits on what can be carried are set out in [Travel and Mounts](../Travel-and-mounts.md#portals-and-express-routes), and together they keep ordinary travel the way goods move, territory is scouted, and military power is projected.

## Landmark Guidance

Each great city has at least one dominant landmark that players use for navigation and memory: a human portal plaza ringed with guild banners, an elven archive grown into the crown of an ancient tree, or a dwarven grand forge visible from terrace after terrace. Each is known by its role rather than a name, and its purpose is to make the city instantly recognisable in play.
