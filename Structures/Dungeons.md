# Dungeons

Dungeons are primordial, indestructible superstructures that defy ordinary architecture. No realm raised them and no vanished empire left them behind: they are grown by mana, formed where one of the world's great currents has pooled and hardened over ages into halls, vaults, and stairs, as [Setting and Lore](../Setting-and-lore.md#the-distant-gods-and-the-source-of-magic) describes the force they are made of. They often look modest from the outside while opening into impossible interior spaces, deep halls, sealed vaults, and routes that do not map cleanly onto the surrounding terrain.

## Design Role

Dungeons provide concentrated challenge inside a game that otherwise emphasises open-world travel. They are dangerous and rewarding without replacing the wilderness. Outdoor creatures can be larger or more disruptive, but dungeons create tighter pressure through traps, navigation, resource attrition, scripted encounters, and escalating depth.

## Impossible Interiors

A dungeon's defining trait is that its inside does not obey its outside. Some hold levels that defy all logic, opening into spaces that feel like open country, a forest or a plain complete with its own sky and sealed off from the world above, where it can be the dead of night on a dungeon floor while the sun is high on the surface. Others bend space and gravity: shifting mazes that rearrange themselves at set intervals and can strand explorers for hours, or floors where the ground curves overhead so a player looks up to see another standing on what is, to them, the floor. Reaching the lowest or highest level of a great dungeon can take hours or even days. The direction a dungeon runs is itself a warning, since some climb and others descend, and the two breed different things. A rising dungeon draws on the high, bright currents of mana, the ones that lift the floating islands, and it fills with radiant, light-aligned things that dazzle and burn. A delving dungeon draws on the deep, cold currents that pool far below the roots of the mountains, and it fills with dark-aligned things that feed on the dark, among them the bound undead garrisons of [Skeleton Knights](../Creatures/Skeleton-Knight.md) and [Skeleton Archers](../Creatures/Skeleton-Archer.md) that hold the lower halls. Not every dungeon guardian is undead, though; maze floors, prison halls, and root-choked chambers can also hold a living [Minotaur](../Creatures/Minotaur.md), turning architecture itself into a weapon for its charge.

## The Threshold

The dungeon's own magic sustains its undead, and that magic reaches exactly as far as the dungeon does. It also keeps their marrow alive. In a living body the marrow makes blood, and in a bound skeleton it never stops: with no heart or veins to hold it, the blood seeps out through the bone and coats the dead in a thick, dark, half-congealed layer, wettest where the marrow is richest, around the ribcage, spine, pelvis and skull. It drips from armour edges and leaves smeared trails across the floors, so a party that watches the ground can read where the skeletons patrol and how recently one passed.

The threshold is the line where that magic ends: a door, a gate, a stair mouth or the opening of a cave. A bound [Skeleton Knight](../Creatures/Skeleton-Knight.md) or [Skeleton Archer](../Creatures/Skeleton-Archer.md) that crosses it, for any reason, loses the binding at once: its light gutters out, its marrow dies, and its armour and bones drop into a spreading pool of the blood it was still carrying. Crossing on its own feet is one way, but being shoved, knocked back or pulled across is just as final, so a doorway becomes a weapon for any party that can move a knight through it. Living guardians such as the [Minotaur](../Creatures/Minotaur.md) are not bound and pass the threshold freely.

## Many Doors, No Loitering

A great dungeon has several thresholds, not one. Some are plain to see, a gate in a hillside or a stair mouth beside a road, and some are hard to find or hard to reach: a crack behind a waterfall, a shaft at the bottom of a flooded cave, a door that only the shifting floors ever bring within reach. Because the floors move, the way out is not always the way in, and a party that descended by one door can surface by another far from where it began. Holding one door is possible. Sealing a dungeon takes a force large enough to watch every threshold at once, and such a force is one that other players can gather against.

A dungeon does not tolerate those who linger at its doors. Its mana stirs the creatures of the country around a threshold and sends them at anyone who stays near it, more of them and more dangerous ones the longer the loiterers remain, while the garrison within gathers on the inner side. Passing through a threshold draws nothing; camping beside it does. By default a group may stand at a threshold for five minutes before the first creatures come, a figure a server may override. The bound undead still cannot cross a threshold, so what comes at loiterers outside is the living wildlife of the surrounding biome, roused by the dungeon and not bound to it.

## Progression

The deeper players venture into a dungeon, the more valuable the rewards become. Early rooms suit solo scouting, lore discovery, and light combat. Lower levels demand preparation, party roles, keys, environmental tools, or knowledge earned elsewhere in the world.

## Rewards

Dungeons reward players with rare materials, spell clues, faction relics, crafting patterns, lore, and high-value treasure. The best rewards pull players back into the wider world: a dungeon map points to a mountain shrine, a relic changes standing with an NPC faction (see the [Reputation System](../Reputation-system.md)), or a crafting recipe requires materials from multiple biomes.
