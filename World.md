# World Design

## Overview

The world is vast and seamless, about 80 by 80 kilometres in a single map, and the whole of its landscape exists while the areas of it open in stages. It is built for long-form exploration and emergent storytelling. A single journey can span hours, with discovery and danger around every turn, and the geography is shaped by one idea: a settled interior, a wilderness that has defeated every attempt to hold it, and a line between the two that is moving for the first time. Players arrive as adventurers at the edge of a frontier that is only now being reopened, and the land itself tells them so. This page concerns the shape of that world; for its history, cosmology, and the central conflict that gives it meaning, see [Setting and Lore](Setting-and-lore.md).

The world holds expansive biomes, seamless travel between major regions, dynamic ecosystems, hidden secrets, and vertical depth across underground spaces, surface wilderness, oceans, mountains, and floating islands. There are no loading walls and no instanced zones, and no map edge marks where safety ends: the change from one kind of country to the next is a gradient that a player reads from the ground.

## Heartland and Frontier

Each of the three peoples is divided among several realms, and every realm holds a heartland: great cities ringed by farm belts, farming hamlets beyond them, and towns and villages thinning out along the roads toward the wild, as set out in [Structures](Structures.md#how-settlements-spread). Heartland ground is worked, walked, and patrolled. Its guards are strong enough that the great cities stay safe havens, and the countryside around them keeps the character of a place that has been lived in for generations. The heartlands are neighbours as much as rivals, and their borders with one another are where the realms' politics play out.

Beyond the last farms lies the frontier, a vast belt of wilderness that wraps every heartland and runs on past them. It was never walled off and no realm ever forbade entry. It went unsettled because it is too dangerous to hold: the elder creatures, goblin war-hosts, and mythic powers described in [Setting and Lore](Setting-and-lore.md#the-wilds-own-powers) treat every village as an intrusion, and settlers who pushed past the last fields rarely outlasted a generation. What changed is the age of adventurers, whose numbers make it possible, for the first time, to hold ground out there. The frontier is therefore both the oldest wilderness in the world and the newest thing in it, and nearly everything a player builds, claims, or fights over happens on it.

## Leaving the Farm Belts

A player who walks out of a great city passes through the whole argument of the setting in an hour or two, and the world shows each step before any interface does. The first ring is the farm belt: tilled fields, orchards, drainage ditches, and farmers walking out to their work, with guard patrols on a well-kept road and milestones marking each stretch. Beyond it the fields give way to pasture and hedgerow, the farming hamlets keep their livestock inside walls at night, and the road is still maintained, though only in daylight does it feel safe.

Past the last hamlet the signs of distance from safety begin to stack. Fences turn to palisades with sharpened stakes, the road loses its milestones and then its gravel and becomes a rutted track, and the chimney smoke that marked every horizon falls away behind. Then come the places where the settling failed: a farmstead with its roof fallen in and a hearth gone to moss, a field returning to scrub, a well-built wayshrine that nobody tends. These are a few decades old at most, never the ruins of a lost age, and they read as what they are, homes that did not hold. Wildlife changes in step. Animals near the farm belts are wary and thin on the ground; farther out they grow bolder, larger, and more numerous, and the first sign of a goblin presence appears as cairns, hung fetishes, and claw-marked trunks well before any camp is seen.

Further still the track dissolves into game trails and the only fixed points are those the wilderness itself provides: a standing stone, a leyline-charged spring, a lair marked by what lies around it. The few settlements out here are the hardy and the lawless, trapper camps and hermit holds, mining outposts, outlaw towns, and bandit camps, and each reads as a place that had to defend itself to exist. At each of these steps the player decides whether to press on, stock up, or turn back, which is how risk comes to grow with distance from safety. [Survival](Survival.md), the [weather, time, and seasons](Weather-time-and-seasons.md), and the creatures of each biome tighten as the ground gets wilder, and a [map](Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md) is only as good as the ground its maker has walked.

## A Frontier That Keeps Opening

The frontier does not stand still. Its edge moves wherever adventurers hold ground: a cleared valley, a guarded road, a claim that survives its first raid, an outpost that becomes a settlement. Each of these pushes the line of safety outward, and the wild's own powers push back, with war-hosts that besiege what has been built and elder beasts that return to ground they were driven from. The edge is a contest that players and the wilderness trade back and forth, and a stretch of country that was a dead end of burnt farms one season can be a road town the next, or a ruin again.

The far reaches open over time as well. The country close to the heartlands is where the frontier is being reopened first, and the deeper and stranger regions lie beyond it, waiting to be reached: the frozen steppe of the [Tundra](Biomes/Tundra.md), the [Jungle](Biomes/Jungle.md) and [Bamboo Jungle](Biomes/Bamboo-jungle.md), the [Scorching Desert](Biomes/Scorching-desert.md), the [Deep Caverns](Biomes/Deep-caverns.md), the [Deep Ocean](Biomes/Deep-ocean.md), and the [Floating Islands](Biomes/Floating-islands.md). They open in stages, as frontier expansions, so that revisiting a region matters as the world grows around it, and [Launch Scope](Launch-scope.md) records which ground opens first. Ground that has not opened is closed, and it lies beyond natural barriers of sea, cliff and mountain wall wherever the land allows, with the in-world barrier of an unavailable region standing where it does not. A player who knows an area well knows it as it was, and the arrival of roads, outposts, and rivals in it is a change the world makes visible rather than announces.

## Biome Variety

The world is made up of distinct biomes, each described in detail in the `Biomes/` folder. The current biome set includes [Ancient Forest](Biomes/Ancient-forest.md), [Bamboo Jungle](Biomes/Bamboo-jungle.md), [Coast](Biomes/Coast.md), [Deep Caverns](Biomes/Deep-caverns.md), [Deep Ocean](Biomes/Deep-ocean.md), [Floating Islands](Biomes/Floating-islands.md), [Forest](Biomes/Forest.md), [Jungle](Biomes/Jungle.md), [Mesa](Biomes/Mesa.md), [Mountain Range](Biomes/Mountain-range.md), [Plains](Biomes/Plains.md), [Scorching Desert](Biomes/Scorching-desert.md), [Swamp](Biomes/Swamp.md), and [Tundra](Biomes/Tundra.md).

Each biome offers its own combination of terrain, weather, resources, landmarks, creature behaviour, settlement patterns, and travel challenges. A player prepares differently for a swamp crossing than for a mountain pass, and the rewards reinforce those differences.

## Exploration and Discovery

Players uncover new areas through travel, quests, map skills, rumours, visible landmarks, and environmental clues. Biome-specific challenges encourage adaptive strategies, while hidden lore and environmental storytelling reward careful observation: the abandoned farmstead, the cairn that marks a goblin border, and the lair whose surroundings say what lives in it all tell a story without a word of dialogue. The express routes, portals, mounts, sailing, climbing, and flight of [Travel and Mounts](Travel-and-mounts.md) unlock more advanced access later in progression, but they add value to distance rather than erase it.

## World Feel

The world feels alive. Wildlife reacts to danger and player presence, NPC settlements keep their routines and trade routes, the [weather, time, and seasons](Weather-time-and-seasons.md) affect combat and travel, and landmarks create memorable orientation points. Whether a player is climbing summits, sailing stormy seas, crossing dense forest, or exploring floating islands, the world is rich, cohesive, and full of possibility.
