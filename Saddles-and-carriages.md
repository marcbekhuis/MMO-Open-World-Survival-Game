# Saddles and Carriages

A saddle is how a creature's back becomes useful, and a carriage is how a creature's strength becomes useful beyond its back. Between them they answer the question every trader, soldier, and traveller eventually asks of a mount: what can it carry, and how much of it will survive the road? A horse in the right saddle is a courier, a scout, a charger, or the pack animal of a small expedition. A team harnessed to the right carriage is a freight line, a coach for a party that wants to arrive rested, or a fortress on wheels. This document sets out the saddles and the carriages that follow from them, what each is for, what each costs, and why none of them ever makes the road safe. The riding rules every one of them builds on are in [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md), and the place they hold among the ways of crossing the world is in [Travel and Mounts](Travel-and-mounts.md).

## Hauling Without Magic

The mage has an answer to the weight of the world. The [Item Box](Magic/Spells/Misc/Item-box.md) carries a store of goods outside the body, beyond the reach of carry weight and out of the corpse's reach after death, and it is paid for in mana, in finite slots, and in the deposit cast a mage must finish before anything counts as stored. Everyone else answers it with a beast and the tack it wears. The non-mage's bulk hauling is saddles and carriages, and the trade is plain: they carry far more than any pocket of magic can hold, and everything they carry is exposed. Cargo on a saddle or in a carriage counts against its creature's strength, sits in the open world where the creature goes, and is looted under the same full-loot rule as a fallen player's body in [Death and Loss](Death-and-loss.md). The more a convoy hauls, the more there is to lose, and the more reason a highwayman has to wait for it.

## Riding Saddles

The riding saddle is the one every rider starts with. It gives a seat that keeps a rider on through a long day, spares the animal the pressure of a bare back, and carries a pair of light saddlebags for the rider's rations, a waterskin, and whatever a day's travel demands. It adds little weight and makes little noise. A rider in a riding saddle can fight with a sword or shoot a bow from the seat as [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md#fighting-from-the-saddle) describes, but the seat is not deep enough to hold through the shock of a couched charge. It is also the saddle a stable lends with its horses.

A riding saddle is made to fit one kind of body. A horse's saddle sits wrong on a wolf and cannot be strapped to a buffalo, and a flyer wears the flight saddle with its harness described in [Flight](Flight.md#flying-mounts) rather than any of the saddles here. Colossal mounts carry saddle structures built under the [Building System](Building-system.md#bases-on-creatures) in place of a saddle at all.

## Goods Saddles

A goods saddle is built to carry. It is a frame with bags or boxes hung from it, sized to the creature that wears it, and the creature's size is what sets its capacity: the goods saddle of a horse holds a rider's worth of provisions for a company, while the goods saddle of a [Large Buffalo](Creatures/Large-buffalo.md) holds a cartload and more. The seat it offers is poor, a perch among the bundles rather than a place to sit a gallop, so a goods saddle is a pack animal's tack, and a rider who sits on one does so at a walk.

Bags and boxes are a choice, and each costs something. Bags are light, quiet, and forgiving of awkward shapes, and they are the choice for a beast that must keep moving fast and light. Boxes are rigid. They keep rain and rough handling off fragile and perishable goods and stack bulk tidily, and they weigh more, clatter against the frame at the trot, and hold their shape even when half empty, so a laden box saddle is the louder and heavier of the two. A merchant chooses by what the load is and what the road will do to it.

Every kilogram counts against the creature's strength as it does for every mount, and an overloaded animal slows, tires fast, and refuses to run. Cargo also changes what a mount can be asked to do. A pack animal laden to the limit cannot gallop away from a raiding party, cannot swim a river it would have forded empty, and is slower to answer the reins than a bare one. The goods saddle's value is the same as its risk: it turns one animal into a small caravan, and the animal is the first thing a robber goes for.

## Armour Saddles

An armour saddle is the war saddle, a deep seat with armour for the mount built into it. It holds a rider through the shock of a charge, the blow that would unhorse a lesser seat, and a rear in the middle of a melee, and it covers the creature in the protection of its tier. It carries almost nothing beyond the rider, because its weight is spent on protection.

It comes in two tiers. **Heavy leather** is boiled and studded hide laid over the chest, neck, and shoulders. It turns glancing cuts and slows a point, adds modest weight, and stays quiet enough for a scout's mount, which makes it the armour of light cavalry and hunting beasts. **Steel plate** covers the chest, neck, shoulders, flanks, and the mount's face, and it follows the rules of plate in [Armour](Armour.md#materials-against-blows): it stops cuts, turns most thrusts and arrows, and spreads a crushing blow, though a heavy hammer still dents it and hurts what lies beneath. It is also the heaviest tack in the game, loud with every stride, and hot enough in the [Scorching Desert](Biomes/Scorching-desert.md) that a plated horse cooks beneath it.

The rest of the rules of [Armour](Armour.md) apply to the mount as they do to its rider. Coverage is partial, and the gaps are the belly, the inside of the legs, the hindquarters, and the joints that must move, so a spear thrust under the barding still finds the animal. The armour sits over the creature's own hide, and layers add up. Its weight counts in full toward what the mount can bear, which means plate on a rider already in plate asks a great deal of the animal, and only a heavy horse carries both without slowing to a walk. Wear and repair follow [Armour](Armour.md#wear-and-repair): a dented plate protects less where it was struck, and mending is the work of the armourer's and leatherworker's stations. The protection is real and so is the penalty, a slower and shorter-winded mount that is heard long before it arrives.

## The Price of Cargo

Everything a saddle or carriage carries costs something beyond weight. **Speed and handling** fall with load: a laden mount accelerates slowly, turns wide, and cannot hold a gallop, and a carriage laden to its team's limit takes a long way to stop. **Noise** rises with it, because loaded tack creaks and clatters, boxes knock, iron tyres ring on stone, and a column of laden animals can be heard long before it is seen. **Visibility** follows: bags and boxes show what kind of traveller is coming, and a wagon with a team of four is visible across a plain and unmistakable for what it is. **Upkeep** is steady: straps stretch, frames crack, axles and harness wear with use and weather, and a laden beast eats more than an empty one, so a long haul is also a feed bill.

**Lootability** is what makes all the others matter. The cargo of a saddle or carriage is not protected as the Item Box's contents are. A mount that dies leaves a carcass with everything strapped to it, a carriage that is broken spills its hold into a pile anyone may take, and a pack animal left standing unattended can be robbed as a sleeping character can, unless its bond makes it dangerous to approach. A mount that panics when its rider dies sheds its packs one at a time as it bolts, each falling where the animal is at that moment, while one that keeps its nerve keeps them on its back, as [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md#when-the-rider-falls) sets out. The honest accounting of any haul is therefore what it earns against what the road is likely to take, and a merchant who moves many times what a mage could carry has made themselves a target in proportion.

## Carriages

A large creature can draw what it could never carry. A horse bearing a load on its back is limited by what its spine can take, but a horse in harness draws a wheeled body over a good road at several times that weight, since the wheels bear the load and the animal need only pull. That is the whole value of a carriage, and it only holds on a road: off firm ground the same load is a dead weight dragged through mud. The animals that draw carriages are the large, strong, steady ones, horses above all, and the heavier the carriage the larger the team. A single horse draws a light cart, a pair a coach, and a team of four or more a loaded wagon.

A carriage is a body on wheels that a draught harness joins to its team, and it comes in three kinds.

A **storage carriage** is the wagon: a long bed with sides, often under canvas, built to hold bulk. It carries far more than any saddle, which makes it the backbone of overland trade in [Economy and Trade](Economy.md), the vehicle that moves ore, grain, timber, and cloth between realms in volumes a pack train cannot match. It is slow, heavy, loud, and confined to the road, and its whole hold is lootable.

A **passenger carriage** is the coach: an enclosed body with a driver's box and room for several passengers. Its riders sit out of the weather, recover stamina as the miles pass, and eat, tend wounds, and rest as they could not on foot or in the saddle, so a company that must arrive fresh, or must carry its wounded home, travels in one. It cannot carry much beyond its passengers' own kit. It also puts a party of people in a box that can be stopped, and a coach halted by a felled tree is a well-known danger.

A **weaponised carriage** is the war wagon, a reinforced body with plating along its sides, drawn by a team wearing armour of its own. Its protected crew fights from behind boards with bows, crossbows, and long spears, bladed hubs cut at anyone who comes alongside, and a heavy prow lets it break a loose line of infantry or a barricade of timber. It is not a siege engine: it does a wall no real harm and carries almost no cargo. Its use is on the field and on the road, where it can hold a pass or escort a convoy and answer an ambush with a fight. Its weakness is its team. A war wagon is only as steady as the animal at its head, and pikes braced against the charge and arrows in the horses stop it as surely as they stop a mounted rider.

## Driving and the Team

A carriage is driven from the box with the same reins and intent as a mount is ridden, as set out in [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md#reins-and-intent). The driver sets heading and pace and the team carries it out, but the whole rig turns in an arc as long as its length, gets under way slowly, and needs a long run to stop, so a driver plans every bend and every halt before they come. Gaits are capped by load: a lightly laden carriage can canter on a road, and a full wagon holds a trot at best. Horsemanship carries over, because the practised driver reads the team's tells early and calms it faster.

Every animal in the team is a creature with its own bond and its own will, and the team is only as willing as its most timid member. When one animal gives the tell, the whole team gives it, and a driver who presses past it gets the refusal every time: the team stops dead, rears, or wheels, and a carriage dragged round at the brink of a ford or the edge of a road can go over with it. A frightened team bolts with the carriage behind it, dragging its cargo, and if the driver is killed a team bonded well to its owner holds steady where it stands, while one with weak bonds runs, taking the carriage wherever the road or the ground takes it. A runaway team is how a wagon and everything in it ends up at the bottom of a ravine or in a bandit's camp. A well-bonded war team charges a shield wall and a poorly bonded one does not, exactly as a mounted charge does, and the driver learns not to plan the charge their horses will refuse. A hired horse is lent for the saddle and is too timid for the harness, so a carriage team is always a team its keeper has tamed.

A carriage with its team gone stands where it is. Anyone may loot its hold, and anyone with a team of their own and a harness that fits may hitch to it and haul it away with whatever it contains, which is how stranded wagons find their way to outlaw camps. The owner's only recourse is to find it.

## Roads, Terrain, and Bridges

A carriage is a creature of the road. Roads and firm, open grass are its ground, and there it runs at full pace. Forest floor, scrub, sand, and shallow snow slow it badly, because wheels sink where hooves do not, and mud, deep snow, and bog bring it to a stop. Steep ground stops it too, since a team that can climb a ridge on its own cannot haul a loaded wagon up the same slope, and a descent is as dangerous as the climb, because a heavy carriage that begins to run downhill overtakes its team. Scree, rock, and broken ground are closed to it. A stuck carriage is freed by unloading it, which takes time and puts its cargo on the ground, or by hands pushing at the wheels, which is a hard lesson in leaving a mounted escort. Fords are crossed at a walk where the water is shallow, and deep water is refused.

Bridges and gates are part of the same logic. A carriage counts its laden weight against any span it crosses, and it is among the heaviest things most bridges ever bear. The realms keep their bridges stout enough for any wagon, while a bridge built by players under the [Building System](Building-system.md#stability-and-support) has to be built to carry one, and a thin timber span that holds a rider may fail under a laden wagon and its team. A gateway has to be wide and tall enough for the rig to pass, and a base that wants its wagons inside builds a yard behind a wide gate.

That dependence on roads is what keeps the carriage from erasing distance. Carriage country is the heartlands of the realms and the trade routes between them, and the road's freedom ends where the road does, at the frontier, where outlaw towns and bandit camps lie beyond the last bridge. The few places a carriage can be driven are also the places an ambusher can predict it will be: the ford, the narrow cut, the bridge, the steep grade where the team slows to a crawl. Convoys therefore travel with escorts, scouts ride ahead to read the road, and the guards of the realms patrol the stretches that carry the most trade.

## Making and Mending

Saddles and carriages are crafted, at the stations described in [Crafting](Crafting.md#stations-and-workshops). Saddles are the leatherworker's trade, with the metalwork of an armour saddle's plate added from the armourer's station. A carriage takes the woodworker's craft for its body and wheels and the smith's for its tyres, axles, and fittings, and a war wagon adds plate on top. Everything is made to fit the creature or team it is meant for. Quality matters here as it does throughout [Crafting](Crafting.md#quality-and-specialization): a master's saddle sits the animal better, wears slower, and weighs less than an apprentice's, and a master's wagon holds together across a harder road. NPC craftsmen in the realms' towns and cities sell plain saddles and plain carriages, so a player who never crafts is fully supplied, while the finest tack and the heaviest war wagons come from specialised players. Everything wears, with use and with weather, and a carriage left standing in the open weathers faster than one kept under a roof. The sheltered yard of a base protects the wagon as much as the team that draws it, and it is as safe as the base is.

## A Wagon at the Ford

The caravan had been on the road four days when the lead horse put its ears back at the ford. The driver saw it a long moment before the rest of the team did, the slow lift of the head, the sidestep, the animal's weight shifting to the bank, and she let the reins go slack and eased the wagon to a halt in the shallows' edge rather than lean on a team that was telling her it did not like what was on the far side. Two outriders went across first on their own horses. They found the water running swifter than the ford had any right to, a fresh scatter of hoofprints in the mud beyond the bank, and nothing else, which was worse. The wagon behind them held a winter's wool for three villages, more than any mage's pocket could have carried, and every bale of it would belong to whoever reached it first. She waited, and the team stood, and the river ran past. She could cross and trust the outriders' count of the hoofprints, turn the wagon on a road too narrow to turn it on, or sit until dark and let whoever had passed ahead of her decide what the night would hold.
