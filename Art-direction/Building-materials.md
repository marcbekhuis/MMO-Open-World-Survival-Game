# Building Materials

Every structure in the game is raised from one of a family of building materials, and each of them has a written brief that says what it looks like at eye level. These briefs exist so that reference images, models, and textures can be made from them, the way a creature file feeds its bestiary plate. The gameplay properties of each material, how much it bears, how it resists damage, what it costs, live in [Building System](../Building-system.md#materials); the briefs here describe only how the material reads to a player standing in front of it. The text is canon over every image made from it: when a reference image and a brief disagree, the brief wins and the image is regenerated, as [Art Direction](../Art-direction.md#shape-language-and-material-discipline) sets out for the whole world. Every brief describes its material as a kit of separate parts, as the [Building System](../Building-system.md#fittings) requires: the bare wall piece, the frame members that dress it, and the fittings that fill its openings, each its own piece, so the look of a finished house is something a builder composes rather than something a single piece carries. Each brief opens its `Reference Set` section with a reference plate in one shared layout, the material assembled at eye height, the bare wall piece from front and rear, the material's frame members and fittings each on its own, and two close-ups, made by following the [building-material-references](../.codex/skills/building-material-references/SKILL.md) process so that every plate in the set is briefed and checked the same way.

## How the Family Progresses

The family climbs from the quick and perishable to the massive and enduring, and the look climbs with it. Thatch and hide are soft, fibrous, and temporary. Timber brings the straight line and the tool mark, first rough and then planked and treated. Clay and brick turn earth into building units, and stone turns the land itself into walls, from fieldstone gathered as it lies, through mortared cobbles and cut blocks, to the fitted masonry of the dwarves, the iron-banded keep, and finally stone laced with mana crystal. Regularity rises along the way: the first materials are as irregular as the hands that gathered them, and the late ones are measured and square. Weight and permanence rise with it, so a settlement's skyline reads as its age and wealth before a single door is opened.

## The Materials

[Thatch and Hide](Building-materials/Thatch-and-hide.md) is the camp builder's material: bundled reed and straw over a light frame, with stretched and stitched skins where there is no grass. It is the quickest shelter and the one that looks most provisional.

[Rough Timber](Building-materials/Rough-timber.md) is the frontier's backbone, whole and half-trunk logs notched, pegged, and left with their bark and axe marks in sight.

[Planked Timber](Building-materials/Planked-timber.md) covers sawn and treated boards: the kit's smoothest timber expression, with visible grain, board seams, and the stain and tar that hold weather out.

[Timber and Plaster](Building-materials/Timber-and-plaster.md) is the work of human towns, a dark timber frame infilled with limewashed wattle and daub.

[Living Wood](Building-materials/Living-wood.md) is the elven material, shaped and grown rather than cut, so that walls and floors read as part of the tree they hang from.

[Clay and Adobe](Building-materials/Clay-and-adobe.md) is earth in the dry country, rammed, plastered, and sun-baked into thick, soft-edged walls that keep the heat out.

[Fired Brick](Building-materials/Fired-brick.md) is clay made durable, small regular units in a running bond, the first material that looks manufactured at scale.

[Fieldstone](Building-materials/Fieldstone.md) is stone gathered as it lies and stacked dry, with every unit different and the gaps chinked.

[Cobblestone](Building-materials/Cobblestone.md) is the first mortared stone: rounded river and field cobbles laid in coursed rows in lime mortar, used for foundations, lower walls, and paving.

[Dressed Stone](Building-materials/Dressed-stone.md) is cut block and ashlar, squared faces and tight courses, the stone of town walls and halls.

[Fitted Masonry](Building-materials/Fitted-masonry.md) is the dwarven mortarless work, massive blocks cut so closely that no blade passes between them.

[Reinforced Stone](Building-materials/Reinforced-stone.md) is dressed stone bound with forged iron bands and straps, the material of keeps.

[Warded Stone](Building-materials/Warded-stone.md) is the late material, stone laced with mana crystal, carrying the restrained cold light the [mana rules](../Art-direction.md#mana-in-the-frame) allow.

## Cultural Variants and Base Materials

A people's architecture is not a separate material but a way of working one. Elven shaped wood, dwarven fitted masonry, and the timber-and-plaster of human towns each have a brief of their own because their surface and joinery differ too much to share one, while the regional and cultural expressions of the other materials, a tarred plank wall in the north or a limewashed one in the south, are treated inside the base material's brief under its variants. Styles and trims chosen at placement, covered in [Building System](../Building-system.md#decoration-and-detail), change a piece's finish and never its material.
