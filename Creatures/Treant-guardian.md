# Treant Guardian

When a warning in the Ancient Forest goes unheeded, the Treant Guardian comes down out of the trunks to drive the offender out. It is the middle tier of [Treant](Treant.md) and the one that does the fighting: 3 to 5 metres tall, between one and two tonnes, walking the forest floor on two root-legs, with two bough-arms that can sweep a party off its feet.

## Appearance and Visual Design

A guardian has four limbs: two arms and two legs. The arms are heavy boughs that grow from the shoulders of a thick, twisted trunk and taper into branch-fingers long enough to close around a person, and the legs are root columns that widen at the base into gripping masses of root. The torso is a split trunk shaped like a ribcage, and the head is a low crown of knotted wood set into the shoulders with no neck at all. Its bark is dark and deeply furrowed, near black at the roots and greying toward the shoulders, with moss, shelf fungus and old nests collecting in the crooks of the limbs. Sap glows in the cracks of the face and in the seams where the bark plates overlap.

It walks at a steady, unhurried pace, no faster than a jogging player, but it does not tire, and it cuts straight through undergrowth that would slow a human. When it stops, its legs sink into the ground and root, and it becomes still enough to pass for a stump or a trunk. A rooted guardian cannot follow anyone, but it heals from the soil and strikes at whatever comes within reach of its roots.

## Encounter

A guardian acts only once the creaking timber and the shadowing saplings have failed to turn the offender, or once someone has struck a treant. It fights with its boughs, in wide sweeps that knock whole groups down, in long thrusts of a branch-fingered arm that pin a single target, and in roots that burst from the ground around a player who stands still. Watch the arms and the face rather than the feet: before each attack the glow in its face brightens, the bark plates slide open over its arms and the boughs draw back, and that is the moment to step aside.

Keeping moving is the answer. A guardian out-walks no one, and the ground near a rooted one is the danger zone, so a fight that goes badly is best left by stepping out of root reach instead of trading blows. Its bark is thick and turns most blades, but the plates slide aside to reveal the wood beneath when the creature attacks, and strikes at the joints where arm meets trunk and on the knots of the face are the ones that hurt it. Fire makes it give ground without ending the fight, and what flame does to the rest of the forest is set out in [Treant](Treant.md). The fight ends when the offender leaves the area or stops the act that provoked it, and the guardian goes back to standing among the trunks. One that falls leaves bark plates and sap resin.

## Story Hook

A settlement wants a road cut through the edge of the Ancient Forest to reach a new timber stand, and sends the players to survey the route. The surveyors have already felled a line of trees to clear their sightlines, and saplings have trailed them for two days. Now a guardian stands across the line of survey stakes and will not be moved, and it has not touched the stakes themselves. Foresters who know the wood say it will let the survey pass if the road is moved, and that a second guardian will stand behind it if the crew fells another tree.
