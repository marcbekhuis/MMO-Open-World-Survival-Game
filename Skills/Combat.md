# Combat Skills

Combat skills are the weapon families a fighter has practised: heavy hammers, greatswords, swords, axes, daggers, spears, shields, bows and the like. They give non-magic characters the same depth of expression that spellcasters receive through [Magic](../Magic.md), and they leave room for hybrid builds. A combat skill adds no abilities on a timer and no numbers to a blow. A weapon's damage still comes from its mass and speed at the moment of contact, as [Melee Combat](../Melee-combat.md#when-a-strike-lands) sets out, and what a skill changes is how well its holder lives with that weight.

## Rank As Technique

Each weapon family has a rank that grows with use: swinging a hammer, parrying with a sword and holding a spear against a charge all train the family they belong to. Rank is technique, in the sense of [Strength, Stamina, and Technique](../Melee-combat.md#strength-stamina-and-technique). A practised fighter wastes less stamina on each swing, aligns the edge better so that fewer blows glance, and recovers sooner once a heavy swing has run its course. Technique never makes a war hammer light, and it never lets a character swing what their body cannot lift; it makes a fighter better at the weapon they chose.

Rank is also what a weapon's requirement reads. A great war hammer may ask for a rank of 60 in heavy hammers beside a value in Melee Force, as [Player Progression](../Player.md#requirements-and-commitment) sets out, so a rank is both a fighter's skill with a family and the record of the time they spent earning it. Ranks have no ceiling, and each one asks for more practice than the last, on the slowing curve described in the [Skills overview](../Skills.md#progression-model).

## Learned Techniques

Practice also teaches a fighter things the physical model can express, never abilities that recharge. A swordsman who has parried often learns to answer a turned blow with a riposte, a thrust launched from the parry itself, using the motion of the guard rather than a new button. A spear user learns to brace the butt against the ground and meet a charge, so that the charging creature's own momentum is what the point meets. A shield-bearer learns to bash, which is a strike made with the shield's weight and size, tiring to throw with a large shield and quick with a small one. Each of these is an ordinary action under the rules of [Actions](../Actions.md) and costs stamina by what it asks of the body. None of them is a lock-on or a command that holds an enemy's attention: nothing in the game forces a creature or another player to look at a fighter, so there is no taunt, and a defender draws attacks by being the nearest threat and the hardest to get past.

## Combat Roles

Combat skills support many identities: agile skirmishers with daggers and light swords, shield-bearing defenders, heavy-weapon bruisers, ranged hunters, [mounted fighters](../Creature-riding.md#fighting-from-the-saddle), and the fighters who stand beside them in a line. No role rests on damage alone. Positioning, timing, terrain, stamina and the weight of what a fighter carries decide each exchange, and rank sharpens a fighter's handling of those things without replacing them.

## Brutality And Finishers

Combat feels brutal and consequential, in line with the game's [Content and Tone](../Content-and-tone.md). Heavy, well-timed blows wound visibly, and killing strikes can resolve as weapon-appropriate finishers: a two-handed cleave that dismembers, a thrust that drops an enemy where it stands. Skill and positioning are rewarded with decisive, readable outcomes rather than a health bar quietly ticking to zero. The force of an impact comes from the weapon's mass and speed, and a fighter's rank in the weapon's family decides how reliably they bring a full arc to bear and how often the edge lands true, tying the spectacle back into [progression](../Player.md), while players who prefer a lower-intensity presentation can scale gore down in [Player Settings](../Player-settings.md) without changing how a fight actually resolves.
