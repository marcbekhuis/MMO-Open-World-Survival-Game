# Skills Overview

Skills define active and passive abilities learned through use, training, and investment. They sit beside character stats from [Player Progression](Player.md): stats describe a character's broad physical and mental strengths, while skills describe what the character has practised enough to do well.

## Primary Skill Families

- [Combat](Skills/Combat.md) - Weapon handling, defensive maneuvers, battlefield movement, and role-specific techniques.
- **Magic** - Spellcasting disciplines and elemental mastery. See [Magic](Magic.md) for spell progression and upgrade rules.
- **Gathering** - Resource-focused skills such as logging, mining, harvesting, skinning, fishing, and salvage. See [Gathering](Gathering.md) for the work these skills improve.
- **Artisan** - Crafting, refinement, construction support, repair, and specialist production. See [Crafting](Crafting.md) for the production system these skills feed, and [Cartography](Skills/Artisan/Cartography.md) for an example artisan-adjacent utility skill.
- **Survival** - Navigation, campcraft, weather resistance, cooking, tracking, and wilderness preparation. See [Survival](Survival.md) for the pressures these skills answer.
- **Taming and handling** - Long-term interaction with trainable animals, mounts, pack creatures, or utility companions where the design supports them. See [Taming and Control](Taming-and-control.md) for how those bonds are built and kept. Horsemanship, the skill of riding well, belongs to this family; see [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md#horsemanship).
- **Social and trade** - Bartering, reputation benefits, settlement services, and guild-facing utility that improves non-combat play.

## Progression Model

Skills improve through repeated use and dedicated training resources. Ordinary play brings steady progress, while deeper specialisation takes intention. Broadly, early ranks bring reliability, middle ranks open new actions or recipes, and high ranks open specialisations that change how a player approaches a role.

Skill ranks have no ceiling and no shared limit. Each rank asks for more practice than the one before, on the same slowing curve as character levels in [Player Progression](Player.md#experience-and-levelling), so the first ranks come quickly and mastery takes a long time. A character can practise every skill in the game, and the only thing that stops them mastering everything is the time it would take; the ranks a character holds are the record of how they actually spent it, which is what the requirements of weapons and spells read.

Unlocks arrive at a readable pace rather than a rigid one. A skill offers small benefits often, such as improved yield or reduced stamina cost, and keeps its larger steps for meaningful ranks. Players can experiment early before settling into a long-term identity.

## Design Notes

No single skill family is mandatory for every player. Combat specialists have ways to survive and contribute economically, crafters reach dangerous materials through trade or group play, and explorers carry information that others value. The system creates interdependence without leaving solo play empty.
