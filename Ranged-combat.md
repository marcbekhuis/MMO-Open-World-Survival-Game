# Ranged Combat

A bow, a crossbow, and a thrown spear all do the same thing: they put energy into a small, heavy object and send it through the air at someone. Ranged combat here follows that object the whole way. An arrow leaves the bow as fast as the bow and the archer's arm could make it, falls as it flies, drifts in the wind, and arrives with whatever energy it has left, to meet armour, a shield, or a gap exactly as a sword would. There is no crosshair, no instant hit, and no endless quiver. What there is instead is the skill of judging distance and drop, the strength to draw a heavy bow, and the patience to wait for the shot that will tell. This document sets out how projectiles fly, how bows, crossbows, and thrown weapons are used, and how a shooter aims. How a blow lands once it arrives is set out in [Melee Combat](Melee-combat.md#when-a-strike-lands), [Armour](Armour.md), and [Health and Wounds](Health-and-wounds.md).

## Projectiles

Every arrow, bolt, stone, and thrown weapon is a physical object in flight. It leaves with the speed the energy behind it gives its mass, gravity pulls it into an arc, the air slows it the further it goes, and the wind of [Weather, Time, and Seasons](Weather-time-and-seasons.md) pushes it sideways. A shot at twenty metres flies almost flat; a shot at a hundred is lobbed, and the shooter has to aim above the target and upwind of it. Nothing hits instantly, so a target at long range who sees the shot leave can still step out of its path.

When a projectile meets something, it is resolved by the same impact rules as a melee strike, with its point as the striking part and its mass and remaining speed as the blow. A bodkin point drives through mail where a sword would glance; a broad hunting head cuts a wide, bleeding wound in flesh and skids off plate; a blunt stone bruises. Armour's coverage and gaps apply exactly as they do to a blade, so an arrow that finds the face behind an open helm does what no sword cut on the breastplate could. Energy falls with distance, so the same arrow that pierces mail at twenty metres may stop in it at a hundred.

A projectile that does not break stays in the world. Arrows stick in the ground, in shields, in trees, and in bodies, and they can be gathered up again, as items in the world under [Inventory](Inventory.md#items-in-the-world); the harder the impact, the likelier the shaft is to snap. Arrows left in a creature are recovered when it is butchered under [Gathering](Gathering.md#hunting-and-butchering), and an arrow lodged in a living body stays there as part of its wound until it is drawn out.

## Bows

A bow is drawn by holding the primary button and loosed by letting go, under the rules of [Actions](Actions.md#holding-to-act): held at full draw it costs stamina for every moment it is held, the aim wavering more as the archer tires, and let go before the draw is near enough to loosing it is lowered with no shot. A loosed arrow ends the action, and the next draw begins with the next press.

Every bow has a draw weight, the force it takes to pull the string to full draw, and the draw weight decides everything about it. A heavy war bow stores more energy and sends a heavier arrow faster, further, and harder into armour, and it asks more of the archer: a draw-weight requirement in Melee Force under [Player Progression](Player.md#requirements-and-commitment), a slower draw, and a higher stamina cost for every second held. A light hunting bow draws quickly and holds cheaply, and is the right tool for game and unarmoured targets. The arrow matters as much as the bow: broadheads for game and flesh, bodkins for mail and plate, blunts for birds and small animals. Each is crafted under [Crafting](Crafting.md) and carried in the quiver of [Inventory](Inventory.md#equipment).

An archer can walk while drawing but cannot sprint, and every step, every wound to the arms, the cold of a winter night, and an empty stamina pool add sway to the aim. A mounted archer's aim sways with the mount's gait, as [Creature Riding](Creature-riding.md#fighting-from-the-saddle) describes.

## Crossbows

A crossbow trades the bow's speed for power and patience. It is spanned, not drawn: spanning is an action held on the primary button, by hand for a light crossbow and with a lever or a windlass for a heavy one, taking several seconds with both hands busy, and letting go part-way loses the progress. Once spanned, a crossbow stays loaded at no cost for as long as its owner likes, and a press of the button looses the bolt at once. The crossbowman's moment is therefore the opposite of the archer's: slow and exposed while reloading, perfectly steady while waiting, and able to hold a shot on a doorway for as long as it takes someone to step through it. A heavy crossbow puts a bolt through armour that would stop most arrows, which is what makes the long reload worth bearing.

## Thrown Weapons

A thrown weapon is a strike that lets go. Throwing uses the swing of [Melee Combat](Melee-combat.md#weight-in-motion): holding the button winds the arm back and drives it through, accelerating the weight as Melee Force allows, and letting go releases the object at whatever speed it has reached. A javelin let go at the top of a full throw flies far and hits hard; let go early it wobbles out and falls short. Heavy throws, a javelin or a throwing axe, are slow to wind and hit with real force; light ones, a knife or a stone, are quick and weak. A thrown weapon lies where it lands and has to be fetched, which makes a thrower's second throw a matter of what else they carry.

## Aiming Without a Crosshair

There is no crosshair. An archer aims along the arrow at full draw, a crossbowman along the sights of the stock, a thrower over the outstretched arm, and a caster along the hand, as a real shooter does, in the first-person view of [UI and HUD](UI-and-HUD.md). The sway of a tired arm, a wound, the cold, or a moving mount shows in the view itself, so how steady the shot is can be read directly. Accuracy is therefore a matter of practice and judgement: knowing where a bow throws at forty metres, how far a crosswind carries a bolt, and when the sway is small enough to loose. The ranged disciplines of [Combat Skills](Skills/Combat.md) steady the hands and shorten the draw, but never draw a reticle on the screen. For players who need it, [Accessibility](Accessibility.md) offers a small optional dot that follows the sway, so it helps a player see where the shot is pointed without making the shot any steadier.

## Creatures

Creatures that shoot or throw obey the same physics. A [Skeleton Archer](Creatures/Skeleton-Archer.md)'s arrows drop and drift like anyone's, a goblin's thrown spear can be watched in the air and stepped away from, and a creature hit by an arrow carries it in the wound. Creatures also read the shooter: an animal that sees a bow drawn on it may bolt before the arrow leaves, so hunting with a bow is about the approach as much as the shot.
