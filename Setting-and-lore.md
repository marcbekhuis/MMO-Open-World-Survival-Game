# Setting and Lore

This document gives the world its identity: where it came from, what powers move through it, and the central conflict that gives every system its meaning. Where [World Design](World.md) lays out the geography and how the world is structured to explore, this file explains why that world is the way it is and what is at stake in it. The short version is a deliberate one: this is a living mythic age, the gods keep their distance, magic wells from the world itself, and the present moment is defined by adventurers pouring into a frontier that has defeated every attempt to settle it, and by the many realms racing to profit from them.

## A Living Mythic Age

The world is young and mythic rather than the ruin of some greater age that fell before it. Its mountains, seas, and floating islands are the work and the battleground of primal powers still abroad in it, not monuments left by a vanished empire to be picked over by lesser heirs. What is called ancient here is primordial: older than any realm, born with the world rather than salvaged from its collapse. The great elder creatures embody this directly, so that a [Royal Dragon](Creatures/Royal-dragon.md), a [Titan Turtle](Creatures/Titan-turtle.md), or a [Stone Giant](Creatures/Stone-giant.md) is less a monster to be farmed than a piece of the world's own myth still drawing breath. Framing the age this way keeps the wilderness genuinely wild: the deep country is a place still being made, dangerous on its own terms, not a graveyard waiting to be looted.

## The Distant Gods and the Source of Magic

Gods are real to the peoples who name them, woven through their festivals, oaths, and dread, but they do not walk the world or settle its quarrels. No deity strides onto a battlefield or answers a prayer with a visible hand, and the design keeps it open whether the gods are persons, personified forces, or simply the shapes a mythic age throws up in the minds of those living through it. Worship still matters, shaping cultures and a handful of sworn orders, but it is faith rather than a transaction with a present master.

Magic does not flow from the gods. It wells from the world itself, from leylines and primal currents and from places and relics steeped in the same force that lifts the floating islands and quickens the elder beasts. A caster draws on the living world, which is why power runs strongest in wild, charged places and why the deep frontier is as rich in magic as it is in danger. This sits directly beneath the mechanics in [Magic](Magic.md): elements learned through use, advanced spells coaxed out of ruins, relics, and rare sites, all of it tapping a world that is itself alive with force.

## The Unsettled Frontier

Beyond the farmland and road towns of the realms' heartlands lies the frontier, a vast belt of wilderness that has never been closed to anyone, only too dangerous to hold. Primal powers and monstrous dominions keep it: elder beasts that treat a village as an intrusion, goblin war-hosts that burn what they cannot carry off, and country that kills the unprepared on its own. Settlers who pushed past the last farms rarely lasted a generation, so the frontier is thinly peopled. Its few NPC settlements are the hardy and the lawless, trapper camps and hermit holds, mining outposts that pay for their ore in lives, and the independent settlements no realm claims, from outlaw towns that shelter anyone the law wants to the bandit camps that prey on the roads leading out. That emptiness is the frontier's promise as much as its threat. Most of its ground still waits for someone able to hold it, which is exactly the room the bases and settlements of the [Building System](Building-system.md) need. It teems with elder creatures, it is contested by monstrous powers that regard every settler as an intruder, and it is seamed with leyline-rich sites, rare materials, and the lairs and barrows of mythic beings. This frontier is the stage on which the whole game is played.

## The Age of Adventurers

For most of the realms' history the frontier was worked only by adventurers, the few willing to take up a trade that killed most who tried it: scouting the deep country, clearing lairs, guarding prospectors, and coming home, when they came home at all, with something no farmer could grow. There were never enough of them to change anything. In the present age there are. Adventuring has become a calling that draws people from every realm in their thousands, and those people are the players. A lone adventurer could bring back a trophy; a crowd of them can clear a valley, hold a road, and keep a settlement alive long enough for it to take root, so for the first time the frontier can be settled rather than merely raided. Nothing in the wilderness changed. What changed is who goes into it, and it is why every realm now courts the adventurers it once ignored. [NPCs](NPCs.md) still take up the trade, but the realms' expansion now rides on the players.

## The Many Realms and the Race Outward

Each of the three peoples described in [Races](Races.md) is divided among several realms, and each realm is true to its people's nature. Human kingdoms push out as adaptable settlers and opportunists, quick to plant a town where others see only risk; elven realms are drawn to the leyline-rich and the primeval, to places where the world's force runs close to the surface; dwarven holds drive mines and forges into the mountains and the deep places, reading wealth in stone the others walk past. Every realm builds its heartland the same practical way, around great cities ringed by farmland and the farming hamlets that feed them, as set out in [Structures](Structures.md), and every realm now pushes into the frontier on the strength of the adventurers it can draw. They are not the only claimants, and the realms are as ready to contest one another as to share the spoils, kin as readily as strangers: two human kingdoms racing for the same mountain pass are rivals long before they are allies. This churn of expansion, claim, alliance, and betrayal is the engine beneath territory, the [Reputation System](Reputation-system.md), trade, and [Raids](Raids.md): not one scripted war but a frontier opening faster than anyone can hold it, where the settlements and guilds players raise through the [Building System](Building-system.md) and [Structures](Structures.md) are themselves part of the push.

## The Wild's Own Powers

The frontier does not yield quietly, and its resistance has a face. Goblin war-hosts, the territorial elder beasts, and the great mythic creatures such as the [Kraken](Creatures/Kraken.md) and the [Flying Leviathan](Creatures/Flying-leviathan.md) are claimants and obstacles in their own right rather than set dressing, and some are effectively powers the equal of any realm. Winning ground means displacing or coming to terms with what already holds it, which is why a regional threat brought low can shift the balance of a whole stretch of frontier. The bestiary that begins at [Creatures](Creatures.md) is, read this way, a roll of the powers the realms are pushing against.

## How the World Tells Its Stories

The game's stories come first of all from play. What players build, claim, lose, defend, and do to and for one another is the real narrative, in keeping with a survival world at MMO scale. Over that emergent layer sits a light authored backbone, the mythic history and the central frontier conflict set out above, carried into play through regional questlines, faction threads, and environmental storytelling that give particular places meaning and a sense of direction. There is no single forced main quest funnelling every character down one path. The [Quest System](Quests.md) delivers the authored layer as regional, faction, and discovery threads a player can pick up or leave, while the larger story is the one the world and its players make together. This is why the setting carries real history and a clear spine without ever becoming a scripted campaign.
